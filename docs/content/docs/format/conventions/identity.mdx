A corpus carries two things that are easy to confuse and must not be. **`dense_id` is where a
vertex is. The subject IRI is which vertex it is.**

## `dense_id` is a gapless `0..V−1`

Across the whole graph — every vertex table together, not each one separately — the dense ids are
exactly the integers from zero to one less than the total vertex count, with no gaps and no repeats.
`V` is the sum of the vertex tables' `record_count`.

- **One id space.** An edge's `src` and `dst` are two integers in it, and a reader needs no type to
  know which vertex one names: the id alone is enough, and the table holding it is the one whose
  rows cover it.
- **A buffer is indexed by id.** A reader that loads every vertex into one array puts vertex
  `i` at index `i`, and an edge list becomes indices into that array with no lookup between.
- **A missing tail is caught.** The declared counts sum to `V`, and the ids cover `0..V−1`; a table
  that stops short breaks the coverage.

Every vertex table is **sorted by `dense_id`**, and its rows are its ids in ascending order.

Guard: `dense-ids` — and every guard named here is specified in full, with what it cannot prove, on
[the guard index](/docs/format#the-guards).

## Each type is one range, in manifest order

The vertex tables take the ids in the order `vertex_tables` lists them: the first table holds
`0..n₀−1`, the second `n₀..n₀+n₁−1`, and so on. Inside a table the ids follow the `subject` in
ascending order. So a table's ids are the sum of the `record_count`s before it, and that many more —
a reader knows which table holds an id from the counts alone, and loads a table into its slice of
one buffer without reading another.

The order carries no geometry. A corpus is the graph, not a picture of it; where a vertex is drawn
is the reader's choice ([where x and y come from](/docs/design/position)).

## The identity is the subject IRI

A new subject renumbers every vertex after it — in its own table, and in every table after that one —
so a `dense_id` stored outside the corpus names a different vertex after the next write.

So the identity is the subject IRI, carried as the column the manifest's `identity` names —
`subject` — non-null and unique within its table. A selection, a bookmark, a link from another system,
a row in somebody else's database: all of them key on the IRI. The consequence that catches people is
in the client: a viewer that tracks selection by *index into its buffers* has stored an address, and
the address does not survive a rewrite.

Finding a vertex by its IRI is a filter on that column — `WHERE subject = ?` over its table — and the
corpus carries no second copy of the rows sorted by identity to make it cheaper.

Guard: `identity-is-the-subject`.

## Thirty-two bits in the column

`dense_id`, `src` and `dst` are **`uint32`**, so **one corpus holds at most 4,294,967,295
vertices**. Unsigned is the half a reader gets wrong in silence: a signed reading of an id past 2³¹ is
negative, and an index built from it points somewhere real and wrong.

Why the column is the narrower of the two widths it could have been is [argued with the measurement
that decides it](/docs/design/corpus#the-width-of-an-address-and-what-actually-runs-out-first).

## At a language boundary

A count, or a sum of counts, may exceed what a 32-bit integer holds, and a `Number` in JavaScript is
exact to 2⁵³. An id is a `uint32` and fits either. **What does not fit is `>>` on a `Number`**, which
truncates to 32 signed bits before it shifts: arithmetic on ids in JavaScript is done on unsigned
values (`>>> 0`) or as `BigInt`, never with a signed shift.
