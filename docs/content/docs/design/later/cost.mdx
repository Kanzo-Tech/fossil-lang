description: Every claim fossil makes about what something costs, as a shape rather than a number, with the measurement that decides it and what it says today. A correct answer looks exactly like a cheap one, which is why this page exists.

> **Deferred, not reversed.** The per-tile cost shapes below have nothing to measure in a
> `fossil/1` corpus, which is read whole; the write-path phases were measured through the native
> host, which is deleted. The code existed at commit `ac1801d4` (main `e5b2113f`), and every path
> on this page is spelled against that commit. What brings it back: a `fossil/1` corpus that fails
> the in-browser thresholds — open over 1.5 s, first paint over 3 s, or heap over 1.5 GB at a
> million vertices over HTTP.

A window that returns the right answer looks exactly like a window that returns it cheaply. That is
not a figure of speech: `open` opened every tile in the corpus on every window for as long as
it existed, answered correctly each time, and eighty-eight green tests never saw it. It was found by
someone outside the repository timing the same rectangle against two corpus sizes.

So the claims are collected here, and each one is written as a **shape** — a relationship to N —
rather than a number. A pinned number would not have caught that defect, because the number was
always correct; only the slope was wrong.

## What the obligations are

| # | obligation | shape | today |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | A window opens a bounded number of files | O(1) in N | met, asserted; opening a corpus too |
| R2 | A window is one query per orientation | constant | met, asserted |
| R3 | A window's cost follows its answer | O(rows) | met |
| R4 | An answer names every gap in it | exact | met |
| W1 | The write path's peak is independent of the corpus | O(1) in N | **not met** |
| W2 | The declared budget encloses the whole write path | — | **not met**, and decided: the number is per stage |
| W3 | Every stage declares what it holds | — | partial |

The reading half is nearly a system; the writing half is a budget around one stage of four.

## R1 · A window opens a bounded number of files

One rectangle covering 1% of the extent, best of three, warm, against corpora this writer produced
at `chunk_size` 4,096 with both orientations tiled. Measured from kanzo-ui with DuckDB-WASM under
`NODE_RUNTIME`, 2026-08-25.

| corpus | answer | files, before | files, now | warm, before | warm, now |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1,000,000 | 812 v / 7,293 e | 245 | **1** | 75.6 ms | **13.6 ms** |
| 5,000,000 | 771 v / 5,316 e | 1,221 | **1** | 1,000.8 ms | **15.2 ms** |
| 10,000,000 | 3,152 v / 34,172 e | 2,442 | **2** | 4,738.5 ms | **64.7 ms** |

Before, the vertex half was handed every tile URL and pruned with a `WHERE`; DuckDB skipped the rows
and still opened each footer. A window reads only the tiles whose boxes meet it now.

**Cold, the obligation is still unmet, and over HTTP that is the whole story.** Driven through the
native `duckdb` CLI with httpfs against a plain file origin — ranges, a 206, no listing, no
coalescing, which is what a bucket looks like from the reader's side:

| corpus | tiles | opening it | one window |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2,000 | 1 | 10 req · 0.03 MB | 17 req · 0.21 MB |
| 50,000 | 13 | 34 req · 0.23 MB | 22 req · 0.40 MB |
| 200,000 | 49 | 106 req · 0.82 MB | 25 req · 0.39 MB |
| 1,000,000 | 245 | 498 req · 4.03 MB | 28 req · 0.42 MB |
| 5,000,000 | 1,221 | 2,450 req · 20.02 MB | 61 req · 0.89 MB |
| 10,000,000 | 2,442 | **4,892 req · 40.03 MB** | **71 req · 0.97 MB** |

**A window is bounded and opening a corpus is not.** Seventeen requests to seventy-one across a
five-thousandfold range of corpus, against ten to 4,892 — two range requests and about 16 kB per
tile, because reading a footer is a length read and then the footer. At ten million that is forty
megabytes and nearly five thousand round trips before a single vertex can be drawn.

**Opening is bounded now, and not by the fix this section argued for.** It called publishing the
boxes the wrong fix and the row-group container the right one, because one file has one footer. The
footer of one file grows with the tiles it lists — 2.24 MB at ten million — and a statement reads it
again, so the writer publishes every tile's statistics once, in a tile manifest per type, and `open`
reads that file and no footer at all:
[the backend](/docs/design/later/backend#the-writer-publishes-what-the-reader-used-to-read-from-footers)
has the ruling and the measurement.

**What would reverse it.** A writer other than fossil's, whose statistics a reader has to check
rather than trust — the check is the `tile-manifest` guard, and a reader that ran it on every open
would be back to reading every footer.

## R2, R3 · One query per orientation, and cost that follows the answer

A window is the scan's tiles and both halves of their edges, and each run of consecutive tiles is
one statement per part — the vertices, `by_source` and `by_target`, each addressed from the same
tiles. The measurements below were taken when a window was three statements in all, which is the
one-run case.

With R1 met, the clock follows the answer rather than the corpus: 812 vertices cost 13.6 ms at a
million and 771 cost 15.2 at five, while 3,152 cost 64.7 at ten — 4.7× the answer for 4.3× the time.

## R4 · An answer names every gap

Nothing truncates, so a gap is an orientation or a relation that was not read, and `edges` names
each beside its batches with a reason. On a corpus whose `by_target` is not tiled, every tile asked
in `dst` declines the relation as `not-declared` — loudly enough that a viewer counted 19–32% fewer
incident edges and could say so.

## W1, W2 · The peak follows the corpus, and the budget does not reach it

`--memory-gib` is a `FairSpillPool` around `execute_graph`, and for a long time it was nothing after
it: `enrich_layout` took the run's `memory_bytes` and discarded it, so three stages sat outside the
pool and a declared 4 GiB run peaked at 8.39 GiB. The number reaches the layout pass now, and is
honoured there by **refusing** rather than spilling — `estimated_peak_bytes` over three numbers the
batches carry, checked before the first tile is gathered. That closes the *declaration* for that stage — and the flag
bounds each stage rather than their sum, which is decided rather than outstanding and argued on
[streaming](/docs/design/later/streaming). It does not close the shape below, and the two should not be
confused. **The peak still grows with N, and a
refusal is what happens when it grows past what was declared.**

The whole build, `/usr/bin/time -l` over the generator plus `fossil run`, unbounded, 2026-08-25:

| N | peak RSS | wall |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 200,000 | 0.56 GiB | 3.4 s |
| 1,000,000 | 3.35 GiB | 20.7 s |
| 5,000,000 | 11.91 GiB | 154.5 s |
| 10,000,000 | **20.72 GiB** | 404.4 s |

For 1.5 GB of corpus. There is no spill path in the pass, so exceeding the machine is a failed run
rather than a slow one, which is the opposite of what [streaming](/docs/design/later/streaming) promises.

**The line got shallower twice, and that row above predates both.** The layout pass on its own was
7.22 GiB at ten million — 0.72 per million — then 3.80 after the Louvain contraction stopped
building one hash table per community, and is **2.71** after the adjacency remap stopped holding an
orientation as Arrow: 0.27 per million. The
`/usr/bin/time -l` table is the *whole* build, generator and executor included, and it has not been
re-run against the smaller pass; what changed underneath it is a layout half that now asks for less
than half of what it asked for. **Re-measuring the whole build is outstanding.**

**And the shape was never intrinsic — but not for the reason this page gave.** It said Louvain's
levels collapse at the first one, ten million vertices becoming 22,171 communities and then 2,736
and 1,365, so that the whole cost is level zero and a level-zero pass keeping only `community[u]`
would hold 40 MB against 0.41 GiB per million. Two corrections, both measured:

- **The collapse is not at level zero on the fixture the budget is calibrated on.**
  `examples/enrich_memory` at ten million and mean degree 14 goes 10,000,000 → **3,757,900** →
  8,832 → 2,081 → 1,487. Level zero stops on the 32-sweep cap without converging; the planted
  communities appear at level *one*. The 22,171 is `examples/layout_memory`, whose planted blocks are
  128 wide rather than 1,000 — a different graph, and the conclusion does not carry across.
- **The 0.41 GiB per million was not the graph.** It was `Weighted::contract`'s `vec![HashMap::new();
  k]`, one hash table per community and `k` in the millions, measured at +3.41 GiB of the +3.82 the
  step billed. That is gone and the step is 0.19 GiB per million.

What was left of the level-zero argument was a measurement that closed it — and the measurement has
since expired; the two paragraphs below are its record and its reversal. The graph *is* still fully
resident while Louvain runs — `read CSR + CSC` is +0.78 GiB at ten million and Louvain holds it for
all 136 s — but the pass's high-water **while Louvain runs** is 3.55 GiB and the pass's peak is 4.72,
reached in the last phase, after all of Louvain's has been handed back. A streaming level zero takes
0.78 off a number that is already 1.17 below the one that binds. **It was worth zero on the peak**,
and a Parquet scan per sweep to get it.

That was the honest end of that thread, and the ceiling it named — the adjacency remap, holding a
whole orientation as Arrow at once — **is gone**. Sampled inside the phase rather than at its
boundary, the +1.95 GiB it billed at ten million was 40% one call: `lexsort_to_indices` over two
`u32` columns routes through arrow's `RowConverter`, which encodes each row into ten comparable
bytes plus an eight-byte offset for a row that is eight bytes wide. A pair of `u32` ordered
lexicographically is a `u64` ordered by value, so the whole orientation is one `Vec<u64>` sorted in
place: **+0.46 GiB, 11.7 s → 6.5, output bit-identical.**

What that leaves is a peak the level-zero argument now reaches. At ten million the pass's high-water
is `community_hierarchy`'s in the unluckier of two runs — 3.94 GiB against the remap's 3.48 in the
same run — where before the remap set it at 4.98. **The measurement that closed the streaming
level-zero question was «Louvain's high-water is 1.17 GiB below the pass's»**, and it is not any
more; re-measuring it is the next thing this thread is owed, and it is measurable before anything is
written.

<Callout type="warn" title="One row of the streaming page does not reproduce">
That page bills `community_hierarchy` at **+0.08 GiB** at ten million after the CSR landed, and
concludes *"the memory did not move somewhere else — it stopped being asked for"*. Run through
`examples/enrich_memory`, the same post-CSR code path bills it +0.39, +0.89, +1.79 and +3.30 GiB at
one, two, four and eight million — 0.41 per million, linear, and three to five times every other
phase in the pass.

What reconciles them is the measurement rather than the code. An RSS *delta* only sees a phase that
makes the process ask the OS for more, and in the full run the pass starts at 6.82 GiB with
gigabytes of freed pages in hand. In the example it starts at 0.24 G and every byte shows. **The
peak falling to 8.39 GiB is real; the sentence drawn from that row is not.**

Both figures are the old contraction's. On the current code the same example bills
`community_hierarchy` **+1.92 GiB at ten million**, 0.19 per million, and it is no longer the largest
phase of the pass — `remap adjacencies + write edge tiles` is.
</Callout>

## What the write path costs, phase by phase

One run of the whole write path over com-DBLP — 317,080 vertices, 1,049,866 undirected edges — with
HEAD's binary in **debug** profile, 2026-09-15, reported by the probe below
(`ac1801d4:crates/fossil-mem-probe/src/lib.rs, Probe`).

**The seconds are indicative and the bytes are exact.** A debug build is nobody's shipping profile,
so what the clock column is for is the *share* each phase takes of it and not the number of seconds;
the byte counts are file sizes and carry no such caveat.

| phase | seconds | share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `prepare vertices` + `collect vertex` | 9.0 | 11% |
| `collect 1 edge table(s)` | 47.5 | **58%** |
| `write_to_dir` — now `write_manifests`, and no payload | 3.3 | 4% |
| `community_hierarchy` | 8.0 | 10% |
| re-read — CSR + CSC, vertices, remap | 1.2 | 1.5% |
| write tiles, levels, index, adjacencies | 13.1 | 16% |
| **total** | **82.1** | peak **0.80 GiB** |

And the bytes, sampled from the destination directory *while the run was in flight*, because the CLI
deleted the staged files when it was done:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| written in total | **115.9 MB** |
| the corpus that survives | **72.6 MB** in 15 files |
| staged — `vertex/Person.parquet` 17.3 MB, `by_source.parquet` 13.0 MB, `by_target.parquet` 13.0 MB | **43.3 MB**, written, re-read and deleted |

That was **37.4% of everything written**, and a write amplification of **1.60×** over the artefact.

**The writer's round trip was a problem of bytes and of correctness, not of time.** It took 37.4% of
what was written and **5.5%** of the clock. The clock is somewhere else: 58% of it is `collect`, which
is the barrier — placing a vertex requires having seen every edge — and no redesign of the staging
removes a barrier. Anyone reading the table for a speedup is reading the wrong column.

### The redesign, and what is left of the table

**The staged bytes are not written.** `ac1801d4:crates/fossil-df/src/files.rs, manifest_files` is the whole
of what the writer emits; the payload is emitted once, by the layout pass, out of the
`RecordBatch`es the executor returns —
`ac1801d4:crates/fossil-layout/src/layout/pass.rs, VertexLayoutTarget` takes them rather than a URL. So the
third row of that table is zero **by construction** rather than by measurement, and what holds it
there is a test asserting the absence: `ac1801d4:crates/fossil-df/tests/sink.rs` fails if a
`vertex/<Type>.parquet` or a `by_{source,target}.parquet` appears beside the manifests.

Two consequences worth separating, because only one of them is measured:

- **The corpus is unchanged, byte for byte.** Two runs of one program over a 20,000-vertex
  self-relation — a vertex pyramid, an edge pyramid, both orientations and the identity index, 11
  files — produce identical SHA-256 digests across the change. That is the property the whole
  inversion had to preserve and it is the reason it could be made at all: the pass was already
  reading, as Parquet, the rows it was already being handed.
- **The rest of the table is owed a re-run.** The three phases that touched those bytes —
  `write_to_dir`, the re-read, and the deletions — are gone or changed, and the numbers above are
  the ones the old path measured. **They are recorded here as a "before" and not as a current
  reading.** Nothing on this page draws a new conclusion from them.

### What bounded the redesign, and the probe printed it

The same run marks `execute_graph — 0.05G in Arrow` against a process peak of 0.80 GiB. That mark is
`ac1801d4:crates/fossil-df/src/lib.rs, arrow_gib` over the boundary value, and the boundary value already
held everything: `ac1801d4:crates/fossil-df/src/lib.rs, GraphArData` carries a `Vec<RecordBatch>` per vertex
type and `ac1801d4:crates/fossil-df/src/lib.rs, EdgeTable` carries one per orientation of every relation. So
the data never left memory in the first place. The trip was **Arrow → Parquet → Arrow**, and the
original Arrow was alive the whole time it happened.

**Carrying the rows in memory to the pass instead of through Parquet and back costs 6% of the
peak.** That was the size of the thing the 43.3 MB was being paid for, and it is measured rather than
estimated, which is the only reason it belonged on this page — and it is what made the answer
"invert the phase" rather than "stream the staging".

## How a claim on this page stays true

**The reader's bill is the tile, and it is published before a read.** A tile's cost is its row
count and its box, both in the tile manifest `tileMatrix` hands back at `open`, so a view knows what
a window will read before it reads it. What a read costs over the wire is measured per statement on
[the backend](/docs/design/later/backend#a-read-does-not-cost-the-corpus), not reported per answer:
`bytes` out of `parquet_metadata` is footer arithmetic and never bytes weighed on a wire, because the
engine reads inside its own process, a Worker in the browser.

**R1 is asserted through the seam, by `e5b2113f:packages/corpus/integration/cost.test.ts`.** The one
capability this package asks a host for is an `Engine`, so everything a reader does passes through
`Engine.query` and the test counts the statements that reach *its own* engine — a wrapper in the
test file, not a mechanism this package publishes. Two corpora an order of magnitude apart and one
comparison: it writes both with the checker's own fixture — JavaScript against the published
conventions, importing nothing of ours — at 20 and 196 tiles, and asks the same rectangle of each,
fixed ground and ten times the density, through `scan` and `edges`.

Four assertions, and not one of them compares against a recorded figure:

- the vertex statements **never name every tile in the corpus**
- they name exactly the tiles the plan chose, and at most two more than the answer landed in — the
  overshoot is a tile whose box meets the rectangle and whose rows do not, which is geometry and
  not a term in N
- **the share of the corpus a rectangle opens falls as the corpus grows**
- a read is one statement per run of tiles, for the vertices and for each orientation

A guard nobody has watched fail is a sentence rather than a guard, and three of the four went red
when the defect they were written against — every tile URL handed to one statement — was put back.

**And one bench, which was not a test, and is gone.** The playground's `scripts/measure-frame.mjs`
(deleted after `2fa5f22`) walked ten written-down rectangles over a bench corpus and reported the
door's ledger per frame, asking each three times and exiting 1 if any cost column but the clock
moved, because a bench that flaps is not evidence. It left with the viewer it ran through; under
[the backend](/docs/design/later/backend) the unit it would measure is one tile, whose cost is its row
count out of the manifest.

The write half has its probe already — `FOSSIL_MEM_PROBE=1` and the two examples under
`fossil-layout` — and what it lacks is the same discipline: W1 is `peak(10N) === peak(N)`, and it is
red, and being red in public is the point of writing it down.

## The instrument, and the three decisions inside it

`FOSSIL_MEM_PROBE` is a crate of its own — `fossil-mem-probe` — and each half of that sentence was
argued.

**It is not in `fossil-base`.** It lived there once, on the reasoning that the substrate is what
sits under both halves of the write path. It is not substrate: it asks the operating system for a
number and reads a monotonic clock, which is precisely the I/O that `fossil-base`'s `System` exists
to hold behind a trait — and it did neither through `System`. The cost was structural rather than
tidy. That `ps` call was the **only** edge from `fossil-layout` to `fossil-base`, so a post-pass
over Parquet linked the whole compiler substrate in order to time a phase. A crate that depends on
nothing is what lets both halves report through one instrument without either depending on the
other.

**The clock is `Instant` and not `System::now()`.** A `SystemTime` is a wall clock, and NTP may
step one backwards mid-run; a negative phase duration in the middle of a memory report is worse
than no report. Nothing in the probe is inside a Salsa query and nothing about it is reproducible
by construction — a timing report identical across two runs would be broken — so the usual reason
to take a clock from `System` does not apply.

**It is off on `wasm32`, and an off probe holds no clock.** `Instant::now()` on
`wasm32-unknown-unknown` is `unimplemented!()` in `std`: it panics with *"time not implemented on
this platform"*, which under `panic = "abort"` reaches JavaScript as a bare `RuntimeError:
unreachable` naming nothing. The probe took its clock before consulting its switch, so every
browser run of `execute_graph` aborted on its first line. What makes it worth recording is that
nothing caught it — `cargo xtask wasm-check` compiles and does not run, and the executor's
JavaScript suite loaded a gitignored `pkg/` that predated the crate by two and a half months. The
switch is consulted first and the clock second, on every platform.
