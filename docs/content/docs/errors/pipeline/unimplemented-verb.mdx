---
title: A verb with no lowering yet
description: "`pipeline/unimplemented-verb` — the stage names a relation verb the catalogue declares and the lowering cannot compile yet."
---

The verb is real: the catalogue declares it for a relation, with a signature, and the checker knows
what it takes. What fossil does not have is the lowering that turns it into an operator of the
plan, so the pipeline cannot compile:

```fossil
Ordered := Purchase.sort(Purchase.id)
```

```text
`sort` is a relation verb the lowering does not implement yet, so `Ordered` cannot compile;
implemented today: `distinct`, `group_by`, `join`, `select`, `union`, `where`
```

Six verbs are in this position today — `map`, `flatten`, `take`, `drop`, `sort` and `count` — and
the list of implemented ones is read from the catalogue, so it moves the day one of them gains a
lowering.

## Why fossil refuses it

The lowering is exhaustive over the operators the catalogue names, and the unlowered six are named
in it one by one rather than swept into a catch-all. They used to be: the old catch-all told a user
writing `sort` that it *"is not a relation verb fossil lowers"*, which was false — `sort` is
catalogued, and what it lacked was a lowering. Two different situations get two codes, and this one
says the gap is fossil's, not the program's. [Not a relation verb](/docs/errors/pipeline/unknown-verb)
is the other one.

A verb arrives with the operator that carries it and a program that spells it, not as a row; [the
algebra](/docs/design/algebra#what-this-does-not-settle) lists the six as what it does not settle
yet. `distinct` is a worked example of why one waits: it lost its `on` columns because without `sort`
there is no way to say which row of a group to keep.

## How to repair it

There is no flag that turns the verb on. Write the pipeline with the six that are implemented, or
do the work before fossil reads the source:

- **A summary per group** is `group_by`, which is lowered:
  `Order.group_by(Order.customer, total = math.sum(Order.amount))`. Its aggregates are `math.sum`,
  `math.min`, `math.max` and `math.avg`; there is no counting aggregate, so a `count` has to be
  prepared in the source.
- **`sort`, `take`, `drop`** decide the order of rows or cut them by position, and nothing lowered
  does either. A cut by position has to be made in the source until they land.
- **A computed value**, which is what `map` would give, usually belongs in the mapping body, where
  any expression of the row is allowed.
