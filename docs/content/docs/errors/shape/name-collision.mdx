A property key in a mapping body is the last segment of a predicate IRI. When two predicates of one
shape end in the same segment, they ask for the same key. Here `shop:name` and `foaf:name` both
want to be called `name`:

The error is anchored on the `type` binding that brought the shape in, and the two full IRIs are
labels on the two lines of the document that declare them. It is reported once per mapping that
produces the shape, not once for every property that trips on it.

## Why fossil refuses it

Both predicates are unwritable while the collision stands — `name = User.name` would mean one of
them, and nothing in the program says which. fossil does not pick, and it does not number them
(`name`, `name2`): a derived name depends on its own label and nothing else, so a small edit to a
document never renames something a program already writes. That rule, and the F# experience it comes
from, is [the naming rule](/docs/design/type-providers); automatic disambiguation is
[discarded](/docs/design/discarded).

The repair lives in the program rather than in the document because the vocabulary may not be
yours: `foaf:` belongs to somebody else, and a local naming problem must not turn into an edit to a
document other people share.

## How to repair it

- **Declare the type in the program.** A declaration writes each field's name beside its IRI, so
  `shop:name` and `foaf:name` are two fields with two names and collide nowhere. The `help` names the
  bound type, the second predicate's full IRI, and a field name taken from its vocabulary
  (`foaf_name` here). [Types declared in the program](/docs/design/declared) argues the form, and
  `docs/programs/declared/declared.fossil` is a program that declares two such fields.
- **Change the document**, if it is yours: a predicate the shape does not need is a line to delete.
  [Naming what you produce](/docs/book/what-you-produce) walks through the rule.
