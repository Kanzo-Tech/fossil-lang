Every non-obvious choice in fossil is a record under `decisions/`, in
[Nygard format](https://cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions): context,
the decision, and the consequences — including the uncomfortable ones. Any choice between
alternatives that took more than fifteen minutes gets one.

A record carries the reasoning, not the conclusion alone, which is what lets it be *reopened* rather
than re-argued. Several here have been, and the most useful ones were reopened by a measurement:

- **ADR-0042 §3** is revised inside its own document by a measurement taken a day later.
- **ADR-0043 stage 1** records being implemented, measured, refuted and reverted — and then, on
  2026-08-05, redone over the API the first attempt was missing, with the predicted −1.7 GB arriving
  as −1.71. Both attempts stay in the record; the failed one is where the definition of done was
  found to be wrong.
- **ADR-0043 stage 4** has a row of its own table corrected against the code: it said to delete
  DuckDB's memory limit as a duplication, and deleting it would have removed the only bound that
  engine had.
- **ADR-0045** opens by correcting two claims ADR-0044 made less than twenty-four hours earlier, and
  then corrects one of its own — a §2 proposal that was impossible, because the crate it wanted to
  merge carries a `compile_error!` on wasm32. Both mistakes were the same mistake: reading prose
  where there was a checkable constraint.

<Callout>
The table below is read from the `decisions/` directory when this site is built. It is not
transcribed, and it cannot go stale — which is the point, because the hand-maintained index in
`decisions/README.md` already is: its last row is 0039, and 0040 through 0045 have landed since. A
record whose file lands in the directory appears here without anyone remembering to add a row.
</Callout>

Status is the record's own field. `proposed` means drafted, not yet in force; `accepted` means in
force; `superseded by ADR-NNNN` means a later record replaced it. The two entries without a number
are decision logs rather than ADRs proper — a spike outcome and the communication record that came
out of it.
