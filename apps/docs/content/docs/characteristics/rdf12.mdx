## Where the spec is, and what that means for us

**Nothing in RDF 1.2 is a Recommendation.** Concepts and Semantics are Candidate Recommendations of
2026-04-07; **every concrete syntax is still a Working Draft** — Turtle 1.2 is dated 2026-07-30, the
fourth draft since May. Concepts cannot advance until one syntax reaches CR, and none has.

<Callout title="So we design against the abstract model">
The model is stable and the syntax is a moving target. The canonical serialisation is
N-Triples/N-Quads 1.2, which carry **only the primitive** — no sugar, nothing that can move under us.
</Callout>

And a dependency trap worth knowing, which turned out to be the reverse of what this page said:
`oxttl` implements RDF 1.2 completely, **behind a feature that is off by default** — but that feature
has been ON in every build of this workspace, enabled by `rudof_iri`/`rudof_rdf` arriving through
`shex_ast`, and never by us. That is why the `Term::Triple` arm in `crates/fossil-df/src/rdf.rs`
compiles: the variant only exists behind the feature. **Our RDF level was a property of rudof's
dependency graph, not a decision** — and the day `fossil-hir` drops ShEx for SHACL, which §8 above
contemplates, the parser would have silently reverted to RDF 1.1. Since 2026-08-07 the feature is
declared in `[workspace.dependencies]`: a no-op for today's graph and a guard for
tomorrow's, pulling in no new dependency. The RDF-star name is gone, and Oxigraph's own changelog says
*"RDF-star support is now dropped in favor of RDF 1.2."*

Base direction goes one step further and then stops on purpose. `"مرحبا"@ar--rtl` survives the parser
whole — `rdf:dirLangString`, disjoint from `rdf:langString` — and **dies at the pivot**, where a cell
is one `Utf8` lexical form and the datatype lattice has no `dirLangString`. It is refused there,
naming subject, predicate and literal, rather than returned as `"مرحبا"`: base direction exists
precisely because the first strong character does not determine layout, so a flattened cell is text a
host lays out backwards with nothing downstream able to tell. The lattice does not gain the variant
until a producer emits one or a shape declares it.

## Three things from the model that govern everything else

1. **A triple term may only appear in object position.** The inductive definition in Concepts §3.1
   has two clauses and both require the subject to be an IRI or a blank node. There is no third.
2. **A triple term has no identity of its own.** Its denotation is a function of the interpretations
   of its three components. What carries identity is the **reifier**, and the spec says so: *"it is
   expected that the reifiers (rather than the triple terms) will be used in further statements."*
3. **`rdf:reifies` is the mediating predicate**, with range `rdfs:Proposition`. There is no
   `rdf:TripleTerm` class.

RDF 1.2 diverged from the 2021 RDF-star Community Group report in four places: subject position was
**dropped**, the mediating predicate was **introduced**, referential opacity was abandoned, and
`{| |}` survived with the same spelling but a different expansion.

**We have no triple term at all, and the one we used to have was from the old design.** The
RDF-star-era `<< … >>` went with the RDF-specific surface: `<<` is not a token, the lexer reads it as
two `<`, and `<` is the comparison operator and nothing else (`grammar.bnf, § DISAMBIGUATION RULES`
retires the rule that once told the two apart). So everything below is a form the language would gain,
not one it has.

## The primitive is one form and one type

```
<<( subject predicate object )>>   ::  Statement
```

And two rules that are **typing rules, not grammar rules**:

- **A `Statement` is only assignable where the shape declares range `rdfs:Proposition`** — that is,
  to `rdf:reifies`. This implements the spec's object-only restriction as a type error, which can be
  explained, rather than as a syntax error, which cannot.
- **All three components must be evaluable over the current row.** A subject drawn from a mapping
  over a different source is an error in the editor. **RML cannot express this rule.**

**A reifier needs no new syntax: it already is a mapping.** It is an IRI with properties, which is
exactly what a mapping declares — one whose type is `rdfs:Proposition`, with its IRI template and an
`rdf:reifies` whose value is a `Statement`.

The sugar mirrors the spec's own hierarchy. Because a mapping body is already structured by
indentation, it needs no braces borrowed from Turtle: a nested block under the assignment plus the
`~` operator that names the reifier — the same operator Turtle 1.2 uses, and free in our lexer.
**And it desugars to a mapping**, so the IR gains nothing: one expression form, one type, and a
source-to-source rewrite.

Two contradictory provenances over one statement are **two reifiers over one triple term** — the
answer the spec itself gives — and in our corpus that is two vertices.

## Where a statement lives in a corpus of vertices and edges

This is the hard half, and it is ours: **no columnar graph format admits an edge as the endpoint of
another edge.** GraphAr's adjacency list is two columns and edges have no id of their own; ISO GQL and
openCypher define `N ∩ E = ∅`, which makes it impossible by definition rather than merely
unsupported.

What the spec gives us is that, since subject position is gone, **we never need an edge id as a
subject.** We need a reifier — which is an ordinary vertex, costing nothing — and a triple term,
which is the object of an `rdf:reifies`.

**So the cost splits in two, and it is predictable:**

| what is annotated | where the statement is | cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a relationship (object is a vertex) | **already a row in the edge table** — `rdf:reifies` points at it, and its identity is its position | **free** |
| a datum (object is a literal) | a column on a vertex; there is no row to point at | **one materialised row** |

That is what forces a **discriminator in the id space**: `dst` stops always being a row of the vertex
table. The reference is MillenniumDB, whose model is exactly this — 8-byte identifiers whose first
byte declares the class, and edge ids dense and consecutive by row position. The width is already
paid for: vertex identity became a bigint in `e385617`.

The alternative — a side table keeping the vertex id space pure — turns **every hop that crosses a
triple term into a hash join**, because a columnar scan has no selective point lookup.

## Three measured facts that fix the encoding

- **Singleton property is ruled out on first principles.** A distinct predicate per statement is
  precisely the pathology that destroys dictionary encoding and RLE, and it broke four of five
  engines in the Wikidata study — their indexes blew up or their queries timed out, because engines
  assume the number of distinct predicates is low. In a dictionary-encoded Parquet column that
  assumption is ours too.
- **Counting triples misleads; budget in bytes.** RDF-star cut triples by 70% while disk grew 3.5×,
  because a composite term defeats the compression a store is built around. With our stronger
  baseline compression the penalty would be worse.
- **Never key a triple term by its `(s, p, o)`.** Three independent sources agree: with that key, two
  parallel edges with identical `(s, p, o)` and different provenance collapse into one and the
  pairing is lost. The mitigation is the spec's own — several reifiers over one triple term.

<Callout type="warn" title="Nesting depth gets an explicit limit, with a number">
RDF 1.2 permits unbounded recursion. **Double** nesting alone broke two of four engines in StarBench,
and Oxigraph — which writes the triple recursively into every index key — has no limit and unbounded
key growth as the result. An explicit cap is an error with a name; no cap is a key that grows until
something bursts.
</Callout>

## The cheap things that must be right from the first corpus

**Base direction on literals.** Datatype `rdf:dirLangString`, syntax `@he--rtl` / `@en-us--ltr`, and
the rule is clean: no direction means `rdf:langString`, a direction means `rdf:dirLangString`. They
are disjoint.

**The version identifier.** RDF 1.2 defines three profiles — `"1.2"`, `"1.2-basic"` (everything but
triple terms) and `"1.1"`. **`1.2-basic` is exactly the profile of a producer that emits directional
literals and no reification**, and is probably our first target.

<Callout title="Deliberately absent">
No triple terms in subject position — that is not our limitation, the spec does not have them. No
capability negotiation yet: nothing is published and no old reader exists, and the trigger is written
down in [the corpus contract](/docs/characteristics/corpus-contract).
</Callout>

## What this does not settle

- **Named graphs.** Design principle D3 says *"RDF 1.2 + quads as first-class citizens"* and **the
  corpus has no graph column** — neither vertices nor edges carry one. That is not missing syntax, it
  is the schema, and it is a live design question: a column, a file partition, or a vertex type, each
  with different consequences for the layout and for addressing.
- **The exact nesting limit** (above).
- **The ShEx check**, which is half-verified. If ShEx does turn out to have nothing, binding the
  target type to SHACL is settled rather than merely likely.
