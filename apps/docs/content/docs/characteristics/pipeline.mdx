## The question this answers

A source almost never arrives with the shape a mapping needs. Filtering, joining, grouping — that
work has to live somewhere, and there are three somewheres: outside fossil entirely, borrowed from
the engine through the source binding, or in the language.

**It lives in the language.** A relation carries verbs, reached through the dot like every other
member, and our checker types the result end to end — which is the thing neither of the other two
options gives.

## But the plan is DataFusion's

This is what makes the choice affordable, and it is easy to state backwards. **We do not need a
planner. We need a checker.**

The typed pipeline lowers to a DataFusion `LogicalPlan`, which already has predicate pushdown, join
reordering and a cost model. We supply what DataFusion does not have — the types of the mapping — and
borrow what it does.

Two consequences, and they have to be read together or the second reads as an amputation:

- **The fourteen MIR operators stay.** They are the pipeline's vocabulary, not an aspiration. This
  reverses the reading that the operator set was over-designed: it was never reached, which is a
  different problem with a different fix.
- **The ten rewrite rules go.**

<Callout title="Removing the rewrite rules is not giving up on optimisation">
It is giving up on optimising **twice**. `rewrite()` was not incomplete — it was competing with an
optimizer that sits underneath it, and losing by definition. What we can do that DataFusion cannot is
type the mapping; what it does better than we will is choose a plan.
</Callout>

## What is true today

**The ten rules are gone.** `rewrite()` ran on every compile and always returned its input, because
all ten matched operators the lowering never emits; `rewrite.rs` and the `eval.rs` that existed only
to drive R7–R10 left together, and `crates/fossil-mir/src/` holds neither today.

**And the source pipeline is the other half.** `Adults := User.where(User.age >= 18)` is a
**relation**, not a value, and it needed three things that did not exist: a source binding that is
not an `io.*` call, `Op::Filter` reachable from source, and types for `where`/`select`/`join`. All
three are there. The opening question — whether `join` is in the first version — is answered: **it
is**, as an inner join.

The lowering emits seven of the fourteen operators: `Source`, `Filter`, `Project`, `Join`,
`EmitVertex`, `EmitEdge`, `Sink`. The checker gained its first two-input rule with them — the joined
row is the two sides' scopes side by side, each column addressed by the binding that owns it — because
a join is where an operator set stops being a list and starts being an algebra.

### The join condition is a predicate, not a key

`on` takes a condition relating the two sides by qualified column, and the two columns need not share
a name:

<Program src="shop/shop.fossil" region="join" title="shop.fossil" />

Three shapes follow from that and each has an executed test in
`crates/fossil-df/tests/pipeline.rs`: differently-named keys plan as an ordinary equi-join
(`crates/fossil-df/tests/pipeline.rs:250`), a compound key is a conjunction of equalities joined by
`and` (`crates/fossil-df/tests/pipeline.rs:278`), and a self-join tells its two halves apart by the
alias `X as Y` (`crates/fossil-df/tests/pipeline.rs:574`).

<Callout title="This page said the opposite, and the rule it stated was never a design rule">
It said `on` was `USING (k)` — one column name on both sides — so `Purchase.user_id` against
`User.id`, the ordinary shape of a foreign key, could not be written. That was a **limitation of the
backend**, not a decision: while the MIR lowering discarded the binding on a column reference there
was nothing to say which side a name came from, and equal names were the one case whose plan could
be guessed. The lowering carries the binding now, and the restriction went with the ignorance that
forced it. Three of the conformance programs — `shop`, `self-join` and `compound-key` — spell the
predicate form and were refused by the old rule.
</Callout>

<Callout title="Deliberately absent">
**No planner of ours.** No cost model, no statistics, no join reordering. If any of that is ever
needed it arrives through DataFusion or it does not arrive.

**No operator the pipeline cannot express.** `Op` has fourteen variants
(`crates/fossil-mir/src/op.rs:43`) and stops there: an operator enters when a verb needs it, and a
verb enters when a program spells it.
</Callout>

## What this does not settle

- **The ten relation verbs the catalogue declares and the lowering does not implement.** `seq/` has
  thirteen rows — `map`, `flatten`, `take`, `drop`, `distinct`, `sort`, `union`, `group_by`,
  `aggregate` and `count` beside the three — and the refusal says so by counting them rather than by
  listing what it knows. Each one that arrives has to arrive with the operator that carries it and a
  program that spells it, not as a row.
- **The three join flavours that are not `Inner`.** They stay defined and unreachable
  (`crates/fossil-df/tests/pipeline.rs:452` pins the refusal). An outer join manufactures NULL, and
  [the shape](/docs/characteristics/types) has no way to declare a nullable property yet, so the one
  that arrives will arrive with that story and not before.
- **How a pipeline's type is reported in the editor.** A source binding today has a schema from a
  descriptor; a pipeline has a schema derived from one, and hover has to say something useful about
  the derived one.
