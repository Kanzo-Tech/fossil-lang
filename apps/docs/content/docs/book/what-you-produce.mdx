Every fossil program begins by naming the document that describes its output. That is where the
property names come from, so it is not an optional line: without it there is nothing to call a
property.

<Program src="shop/shop.fossil" region="shapes" title="shop.fossil" />

Three things happen on that line. The document is read. Two of its shapes are pulled into scope under
the names you chose. And from then on `Person` and `Order` are types, with a known set of properties,
a known kind of value on each, and a known number of them allowed.

The bare keys `label` and `price` come out of `sh:path shop:label` and `sh:path shop:price` exactly

you get has the same three things on every property: a name, a kind of value, and a count.

## Binding is by position

The names on the left are yours. They bind to the shapes **in the order the document declares them**,
not by matching what they are called.

<Program src="shop/shop.fossil" region="shapes" title="shop.fossil" />

`Person` is the first shape in `shop.shex`, `Order` is the second. Call them `Buyer` and `Sale` and
the output is byte for byte the same, because the IRI written into the graph is the one in the
document. What the local name is for is the rest of your program: the mapping header that says
`from`, and the diagnostic that has to name something.

That is what lets two documents live in one program without arguing:

It also gives you a check, and it is worth knowing exactly which one. Name three members of a
document that declares two and the third binds nothing — the Nth name takes the Nth shape, and there
is no third shape to take. That is an error, reported at the **use** of the surplus name rather than
at the binding line:

```
`Extra` is declared and bound nothing: the binding names 3 shape(s) and the
document declares 2. Names bind by POSITION, so there is no shape 3 for it
to take.
```

Which means a surplus name you never use is silent. The other direction is not an error at all:
naming two members of a document that declares five binds the first two and ignores the rest.

And it has a cost, stated plainly: reorder the shapes inside the document and the program rebinds.
Shape documents are files you version and review, which is what makes that acceptable, but it is
worth knowing the day you go and tidy one up.

## What a name looks like once you have it

A property key in a mapping body is a bare word: **the last segment of the predicate IRI**.
`https://shop.example/voc#email` is `email`. `http://xmlns.com/foaf/0.1/name` is `name`.

The rule is narrow on purpose. The name depends on the label and nothing else — not on how many of
the property there may be, not on what kind of value it holds, not on where it sits in the document.
There is no plural-to-singular guesswork, and there is no numbered suffix. A small edit to a document
never renames something you were already writing.

not turn into an edit to a document other people share.
