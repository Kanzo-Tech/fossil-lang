There are two ways to read a graph you cannot see all of, and the decision here is to build both and
**never cross them**.

| | what it is | when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **cosmos.gl** | the whole graph on the GPU, pan and zoom freely | it fits |
| **holons** | a mipmap of aggregates, you descend and ascend | it does not |

A mode that switches between the two as the camera zooms was built far enough to be rejected, and
[what was discarded](/docs/design/discarded) carries why. The short form: the zoom level at which
the reader is most lost is the one where the interaction changes under them.

The corpus serves both without knowing about either — cosmos.gl reads the payload,
a holon reader reads the tree. This page is the second one.

## The partition is arbitrary. The aggregation is exact.

That sentence governs everything below, and it is narrower than the one this page used to make.

**A reader may not conclude *these vertices belong together*.** Whatever put two vertices in the same
holon — a community detection run, a grid, a sort — is a choice made by a writer, and nothing in the
corpus certifies it as structure in the world.

**A reader may conclude *this region holds exactly N vertices and M edges leave it*.** That is an
arithmetic fact about the rows below, it is falsifiable against them, and it is the only thing a
holon is allowed to assert.

The weaker promise is what makes the three obligations further down **necessary rather than
optional**. If the grouping carried meaning, a reader could take the writer's word for the counts as
well; because it carries none, the counts are the entire content of a rung, and a rung whose counts
do not reconcile against the level below is not a coarser view of anything.
`crates/fossil-layout/tests/holons.rs` does not change a line for this page's change of mind, and
that is the test: what it checks is the aggregation, which is the half that was never arbitrary.

## A holon is a texel, and the corpus is already a mipmap

The format did not have to be argued into this shape. It is in it.

`crates/fossil-sinks/src/manifest.rs, TILE_SHIFT` is 12, and 4,096 is 4⁶.
`crates/fossil-sinks/src/manifest.rs, STRIDE_BITS` is 2, so
`crates/fossil-sinks/src/manifest.rs, stride` is `4^k` and a level's address is the payload's shift
plus `2k`. And `dense_id` is not a name a writer chose: it is a vertex's **rank in the Morton order
of its own position**, which is what `crates/fossil-layout/src/layout/morton.rs, morton_ranks`
computes and what
[the order that makes an interval mean something](/docs/format/conventions/addressing#the-order-that-makes-an-interval-mean-something)
specifies. `crates/fossil-sinks/src/manifest.rs, tile_of` states the consequence in its own doc:

> tile `i` **is** the range `[i·4096, (i+1)·4096)`, its parent is a further shift, and the lowest
> common ancestor of two vertices is the common prefix of their ids.

So **a cell at any depth is a contiguous interval of `dense_id`** — exactly, not approximately, and
without a tree on disk to walk. That is a mipmap's addressing, arrived at for a transport reason and
then true for a rendering one.

What the analogy then demands is a distinction the page did not have a word for:

| | what it holds | what is equal across it | who reads it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **texel** | a summary of a region | **area** | the camera — one mark per texel |
| **tile** | the rows themselves | **count** | the transport — one range request per tile |

Both are intervals of the same `dense_id` axis, and as the writer cuts them **they nest**: a cell id
is `dense_id >> shift` and nothing else — `crates/fossil-layout/src/layout/cells.rs, Pyramid` — so a
cell boundary falls at every `4^k`-th vertex and a tile boundary at every 4,096th, which is `4^6` of
the same axis. The page said neither nested inside the other, and the tree says otherwise.

Which puts the weight of the table on one word. **A cell is cut by count; what has to make it an
area is the placement.** Nothing in this pyramid measures a region of the plane — it takes a fixed
number of consecutive ranks and summarises them — and that is a texel only if the plane those ranks
came from was filled at one density. A reader that conflates the two rows above gets a mark whose
size means nothing; a *writer* that fills the plane unevenly gets the same thing, one layer earlier.

## What makes a cell a region of the plane, and it is not this page

The addressing above is exact and the geometry under it is borrowed. `dense_id` is a rank in the
Morton order of a position, so an interval of ids is an interval of the Z-curve — a compact run,
always — but the **area** that run covers is whatever the placement put there, and the placement
today is `crates/fossil-layout/src/layout/place.rs, cluster_layout` on a grid whose pitch is fixed
by the largest group.

Measured over com-DBLP, that cost the analogy its subject. At the declared base of sixteen vertices
per cell, a rung-1 cell ran from 4,676 square units to 4,764,880,428 — **1,019,008×, at one rung, at
one count** — because sixteen consecutive ids inside the 20,459-member group are sixteen members of
one phyllotaxis disc and sixteen out where the median group is are five whole groups in five whole
grid cells. A mipmap level whose texels differ in area by six orders of magnitude is not a mipmap
level, and `corpus.bnf` was calling a cell row *one texel: a region of the plane, summarised* on the
strength of an addressing argument that never reached the plane.

What repaired it is a change to the placement and to nothing here:
[a cell per group](/docs/design/position) gives every group a block of its own size, which makes the
density uniform, which makes a rank interval an area interval. The same rung now runs 4,911 to
34,618,064 — **7,049×** — and the coarse rungs, where a mark actually stands for a region, land at
302× by rung 3 and 11× by rung 6. What is left is bounded and named over there: a power-of-four
rounding, and the Z-curve's own jumps.

**The pyramid does not change shape.** Same base, same `ceil(V / 4^k)` per rung, same files, same
columns, and the same `dense_id >> shift` computing a cell id. It changes what one of its rows is
about, which is the only part of it a reader was ever going to see.

And the pyramid is already the other half of a mipmap. A level in
`crates/fossil-sinks/src/manifest.rs, VertexLevels` is every vertex whose `dense_id` is a multiple of
`4^k` — one **representative** per block. A rung is the **aggregate** of the same block. Same
address, two answers to two questions, which is why one cannot be spelled as the other.

## A holon is not an entity, and no reader is in a position to ask

The attractive version of this gives each community a type and an IRI — `Person@l1`, addressable,
shaped, a first-class subject. It is rejected, and the page used to reject it on identity: a
community does not exist in the source, hashing the member set changes the moment one author joins,
a counter is not stable across reorderings, and every reference dangles.

That argument still holds, and under *the partition is arbitrary* it no longer has to be made.
**Nothing but the camera reads the tree.** `crates/fossil-graph/src/operations/mod.rs, Verb` is the
six-verb surface, and none of them descends a rung: `read`, `expand` and `path` return **vertices**,
because a holon is not one, and a verb that returned a holon would be answering a question about the
plane in a language about the graph. That is the split the door already draws between its addressed
half and its queried half — the camera computes addresses and asks for bytes, the verbs run SQL —
and `crates/fossil-graph/src/lib.rs` states it as *no verb draws; the camera is addressed, not
queried*. [One door](/docs/design/one-door) is where the seam itself is argued.

So the question *is a holon an entity?* disappears without being answered, which is where the
identity argument in [what was discarded](/docs/design/discarded) ran aground: it had to say what a
holon **is**, and there was no answer that survived an incremental write. Saying who reads it costs
nothing and settles the same thing.

What survives is the small version, and it is enough: **a row** carrying a group, its position, how
many members it has, and its parent. No IRI, no shape, no new vertex type, nothing that pretends to
be a thing in the world. [Identity](/docs/design/identity) is the page that says what a subject is
here, and a holon is deliberately not one.

The row's columns are declared per rung, in `crates/fossil-sinks/src/manifest.rs, HolonRung`, and
**the model reserves no spelling for any of them**. A payload property is carried through opaquely,
so a reader never has to know its name; these are roles a reader acts on — descend by the parent,
size a mark by the member count, check mass with the internal weight — and what says which is the
declared list rather than a convention a second writer could spell differently.

## It is a third artefact, and the tree already ruled on that

The obvious home for a holon tree is `levels:` — the pyramid is already a hierarchy, the addressing
is already arithmetic, and a holon level looks like a coarse level. It cannot go there, and the
ruling is not made on this page. [The reference viewer](/docs/design/reference-viewer) states the
model's one load-bearing decision and what contradicts it:

> The model is one decision deep, and it is the decision to make a level a **subset of real rows**
> rather than a summary. […] That would be a **third** artefact and not a replacement — a level that
> answers a different question under a different name, which is the one thing the one-contract
> invariant forbids doing under the same one.

A level in `crates/fossil-sinks/src/manifest.rs, VertexLevels` is every vertex whose `dense_id` is a
multiple of `4^k`. Every row is a real vertex at the position the payload gives it; nothing is
synthesised. A holon is a synthetic row by construction. Putting one in a `Projection` would make
`scale:` mean two different things in the same document, which is exactly the failure the
one-contract rule exists to prevent. **Being a texel over the same cells does not soften that** — it
sharpens it, because now the two blocks would share an address and disagree about what a row is.

So: its own block, its own prefix, its own name. It is
`crates/fossil-sinks/src/manifest.rs, HolonTree`, and it hangs off a vertex type beside `index:` and
`coordinates:` rather than inside `projections:`. A type declares one with
`crates/fossil-sinks/src/manifest.rs, with_holons`.

| the block declares | because |
| --- | --- |
| a `prefix`, and a `path` per rung | a tile's URL is the one part a reader cannot compute, and there is no listing over HTTP |
| a `chunk_size` of its own | tile `k` of a rung is the `k`th run of **holon** rows, and a rung is not as long as the payload — the number that slices it is its own even where the two arithmetics agree |
| a `holon_count` per rung | the sibling of `vertex_count`: a hole in the middle is caught by the addressing and a missing tail is caught by nothing |
| the `relations` it was computed over | mass conservation has no population without them, and a type may be the source of several |
| `properties` per rung | a group id, a position, a member count, a parent and an internal weight are columns that exist nowhere else in the corpus |
| `coordinates` | the one thing on a holon that is chosen rather than aggregated |

<Callout type="warn">
**That second row said *a holon id is not a `dense_id`*, and the premise it rested on is gone.** The
argument was `crates/fossil-sinks/src/manifest.rs, VertexIndex`'s, one artefact along: an index is a
second **order** over the payload's rows, so its tile `k` has nothing to do with the payload's tile
`k`, and reusing the payload's number would read as an alignment that does not exist. Carried across
to a holon, it assumed group ids are whatever a partitioning algorithm handed back — arbitrary
labels with no relation to the id space.

Under a quaternary partition they are not arbitrary. **Holon `r` of rung `k` covers exactly
`[r·4^k, (r+1)·4^k)`**, so a holon id is `dense_id >> 2k` — `tile_of` with a different shift — and
the alignment the argument denied is the whole mechanism. What survives is the weaker reason the row
now gives: a rung is a different *length* from the payload, so it slices at its own number.

`VertexIndex`'s own argument is untouched, because an index really is a second order and a rung is
not.
</Callout>

**And no `scale`, which is the difference doing work rather than a field left out.** A projection's
scale is arithmetic a reader spends — rows are `count.div_ceil(scale)` and a tile address is a shift
by `log2(scale)`. A rung declares **how many holons it has** and a reader divides if it wants a
ratio, which is what lets one manifest describe a tree cut out of a dendrogram (where the
contraction is measured after the fact, and is 5.69× where the exponent says 4) and a tree over
cells (where it is 4 exactly) without the document meaning two things.

## What Louvain actually emits is not a holarchy

The tempting shortcut is to publish the levels the community detection already found. Measured over
com-DBLP — 317,080 authors, 1,049,866 undirected co-authorship edges — those levels are:

| level | communities | contraction vs. below | p50 | p90 | max | modularity | quotient mean degree |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 55,712 | 5.7× | 3 | 8 | 20,459 | 0.6356 | 5.7 |
| 2 | 9,248 | 6.0× | 13 | 45 | 29,720 | 0.7782 | 11.6 |
| 3 | 1,696 | 5.5× | 36 | 231 | 30,700 | 0.8057 | 23.5 |
| 4 | 690 | 2.5× | 12 | 910 | 30,722 | 0.8091 | 26.0 |
| 5 | 595 | **1.2×** | 10 | 1,355 | 30,722 | 0.8092 | 23.2 |

Four things in that table disqualify it as a navigable tree, and none of them is a flaw in Louvain
— it was not solving this problem.

**The contraction collapses.** 5.7×, 6.0×, 5.5×, then 2.5×, then 1.2×. The last level buys almost
nothing: 690 groups become 595, and modularity moves by 0.0001. A reader ascending that tree takes a
step that changes nothing on screen.

**The levels are wildly unbalanced within themselves.** At level 5, the median group has 10 members
and the largest has **30,722** — 9.7% of the entire graph in one node, beside groups of ten. That
is not a level of detail; it is a level of detail for some of the graph and the raw graph for the
rest.

**The finest level is very nearly the graph itself.** 55,712 groups over 317,080 vertices, median
size 3, and **53.5% of them have three members or fewer**. Nothing is summarised.

**And it does not break when you zoom, which is the one a reader feels.** Read the `max` column
downwards — 30,722, 30,722, 30,700, 29,720, 20,459. Descending from the top, the largest group keeps
**100%, 99.9%, 96.8% and 68.8%** of itself at each step. You zoom into the biggest blob on the
screen, the one holding 9.7% of the graph, and for four steps nothing happens: the group does not
break up, it *follows you down*. A cell splits into four. Every time, at every depth, whatever the
data does. That is the difference between a level of detail and a lucky one, and it is the argument
that decides between the two partitions rather than a preference between them.

Where the structure genuinely is: modularity climbs from 0.6356 to 0.8092 and the levels whose
quotient is still sparse enough to lay out are **2 and 3** — 9,248 and 1,696 groups at mean quotient
degree 11.6 and 23.5. That is the measurement `crates/fossil-layout/examples/hierarchy_stats.rs, read_edges`
exists to produce, and it says the hierarchy is real and its published levels are an accident of
where the algorithm's sweeps happened to stop.

## The corpus carries one multiscale structure, and it is the cell pyramid

The question this page has carried open — a pyramid of cells, or a holarchy cut out of the
dendrogram — is closed. **A corpus publishes the cell pyramid under `holon/r{k}/` and does not
publish a holarchy beside it.** [What was discarded](/docs/design/discarded) carries the holarchy
with what it would have bought and what would bring it back.

**The argument is not cost**, and saying so first is most of the value of writing it down. A rung's
cells and a cut's groups land within an order of magnitude of each other at every comparable scale,
and neither a byte count nor a row count that close decides anything. What decides it is that **the
reader has to stay arithmetic.**

A cell is `dense_id >> shift` and nothing else —
`crates/fossil-layout/src/layout/cells.rs, Pyramid` — so a reader holding a vertex holds its cell at
every rung with no listing, no fetch and no table, and
`crates/fossil-sinks/src/manifest.rs, holons_at` declares `ceil(V / 4^k)` for the whole tree before
a byte is written. **A holarchy's groups have measured extents.** They are wherever Louvain's sweeps
stopped, they are different for every corpus, and no arithmetic over `vertex_count` produces them —
so a reader that wanted to address one would first have to fetch the boundaries a heuristic found.
That pushes the heuristic across the corpus boundary and into the read path, where every reader pays
for it and no reader can check it. `/docs/format` has no listings and no discovery request for
exactly that reason, and a second structure that needed one would be the first thing in the corpus
to break the rule the rest of it is built on.

### Neither partition is blind to the graph, and the question was framed as if one were

This page put the choice as *does it organise by region, blind to the graph, or by community?* **That
framing is false**, and it is retracted here rather than dropped, because it is the framing a reader
arrives with. **Both structures cut the same dendrogram.**

`crates/fossil-layout/src/layout/community.rs, order_by_hierarchy` numbers the placement groups by a
depth-first walk of the hierarchy, so a run of consecutive group ids is a subtree; the buddy frontier
on [where x and y come from](/docs/design/position) turns that into a run of consecutive Morton
codes, hence a contiguous interval of `dense_id`. Measured over com-DBLP by
`crates/fossil-layout/examples/hierarchy_stats.rs, plane_report`: **55,712 of 55,712 placement groups
fall in a single run of `dense_id`, and the id axis is monotone in group order.** Every coarser level
of the dendrogram is therefore a contiguous interval as well, and a cell — a fixed count of
consecutive ranks — is a window onto that same order rather than onto something orthogonal to it.

`corpus.bnf` already says the half of this that reaches the plane, in the comment over the pyramid's
own columns: a cell «is an area only because every group is placed in a block sized to its own
membership». The graph is in the addressing before any rung is cut.

**The real difference is where the cut falls — at a fixed count, or at a semantic frontier.** That
is a far smaller difference than *blind or not*, and it is the one the rest of this section measures.

### The holarchy does not reach further, and the margin is six rungs nothing asks for

If a cut of the dendrogram is not the one that sees the graph, the argument left for it was depth:
it descends *and* it ends somewhere the data chose. Measured over com-DBLP, it ends soonest of the
three coarse artefacts this corpus has:

| how far a coarse view descends on com-DBLP | its floor |
| --- | --- |
| a holarchy, `crates/fossil-layout/src/layout/community.rs, Cut` at factor 4 and chunk 4,096 | **1,696 groups** — levels 4 and 5 are dropped, neither being a quarter of the one above it |
| the level pyramid, at its one-tile floor | **1,239 marks** |
| the cell pyramid, at the rung holding a single cell | **1 cell** — 19,818 → 4,955 → 1,239 → 310 → 78 → 20 → 5 → 2 → 1, and every rung of that list carries a picture |

**The holarchy stops above the level pyramid's own floor**, and six rungs above the cell pyramid's.
A structure whose remaining attraction was semantic depth reaches the coarse end last of the three.

<Callout type="warn">
**This paragraph was narrowed to «by one step rather than by the six the emitted rung list appears to
give», and the narrowing is withdrawn. Six is right.** The narrowing rested on a fidelity
measurement that was an artefact of its own ruler: total variation was taken on a fixed 48×48 grid,
where rung 4's 310 marks are asked to fill 2,304 bins, both the rung and its null pin against TV 1,
and what is left between them is binning noise.
[A fidelity measure has a resolution, and below one bin per mark it is measuring itself](/docs/design/camera#a-fidelity-measure-has-a-resolution-and-below-one-bin-per-mark-it-is-measuring-itself)
is the general form of the trap. Re-measured on a grid fitted to each arm — about `sqrt(marks)` bins
per axis, so nothing fills more bins than it has points —
`crates/fossil-layout/tests/level_vs_rung.rs, fidelity` puts rung 4 at **0.2230 against a null of
0.3700**, a margin of **+0.1470**, wider than rung 1 gets on the old ruler (+0.0784). The margin
**grows** down the pyramid rather than decaying: +0.0956, +0.1168, +0.1318, +0.1470, +0.1814,
+0.2543, +0.3341 through rung 7.

**So reach is restored as a measurement, and it still is not much of an argument — for a reason that
has nothing to do with fidelity.** A camera spends a budget in *marks*, and the smallest budget
anything in this tree spends is **15,000** — `SCREEN_MARKS`, the number the base of the pyramid is
picked with; `apps/playground/src/tiles.ts` spends `BOUNDED_DEFAULTS.limit`'s 20,000, and a 1,200 px
canvas at three pixels a mark would ask 160,000. Only **rung 1, at 19,818, is above that floor**, and
the holarchy's 1,696 groups is below it too. The extra six rungs are a margin in a region nothing
reads. What the decision above rests on is untouched either way: a cell is `dense_id >> shift` and a
group's extent is measured, and no depth measurement moves that.
</Callout>

It cannot be cut differently into something deeper. The cut chooses out of the partitions the
dendrogram already holds, and getting nearer to four would need levels *between* Louvain's —
[the branching factor is declared, not inherited](/docs/design/cells#2-the-branching-factor-is-declared-not-inherited)
is where that is argued, and why it is not a tuning knob.

### And the level it would publish is not a level of detail

The level a holarchy would hand a reader as its coarse partition is Louvain's third:
`crates/fossil-layout/src/layout/community.rs, flatten_to_budget` walks up until a level fits
`crates/fossil-layout/src/layout/place.rs, CLUSTER_BUDGET`, and 2,048 admits level 3's 1,696 groups
where level 2's 9,248 do not. Its group sizes, and the two levels above it:

| level | p50 | p90 | max | the max, as a share of the graph |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | **36** | 231 | **30,700** | **9.7%** |
| 4 | 12 | 910 | 30,722 | 9.7% |
| 5 | 10 | 1,355 | 30,722 | 9.7% |

One mark would stand for a tenth of the whole graph while the median mark stands for thirty-six. And
ascending does not repair it — **the median falls while the maximum holds**: 36 → 12 → 10 against
30,700 → 30,722 → 30,722, which is within twenty-two vertices of standing still. So the small groups
get *smaller* relative to the largest with every step up, which is the opposite of what a level of
detail does. A rung's cells hold `4^k` vertices each, by
construction, at every rung and on every corpus.

### What the arithmetic cut already captures, derived rather than measured

The monotonicity above has a consequence worth stating, and worth labelling **derived**: nobody has
measured the written `purity` column over com-DBLP, and this is not that measurement.

Each of the 1,696 level-3 communities is a contiguous interval of `dense_id`, so the partition has
**1,695 boundaries**. A cell is impure only if it contains one, and a boundary falls inside at most
one cell at a given rung — so **impure cells ≤ 1,695, at every rung**, whatever the data does.
Evaluated against the rung counts:

| rung | cells | perfectly pure, at least |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 19,818 | **91.4%** |
| 2 | 4,955 | **65.8%** |
| 3 | 1,239 | the bound is exhausted — 1,239 is fewer than 1,695 |

That is an arithmetic bound on a measured monotonicity and nothing more. The true figure is higher
wherever a community boundary lands where a cell boundary already is, and the bound says nothing at
all from rung 3 down. What would replace it is a count over the written Parquet, which is the same
recount `crates/fossil-layout/tests/cells.rs` already performs on a fixture.

**And this is what `mode` and `purity` are for.** They read as an awkward bridge — a categorical
summarised by a structure that did not choose it — and they are the opposite: the number that says
**how much of the semantic partition the arithmetic cut already captured**. A rung that publishes
them publishes the holarchy's own content, per cell, as a measurement beside the counts rather than
as a second tree a reader would have to fetch in order to address anything.

## The base of the pyramid is a parameter, not a property

A mipmap over an image needs no such field: level 0 is every pixel, the image is full by
construction, and each level is a quarter of the one below it all the way down. **A set of points is
never full**, and the whole difference between the two lives at the base.

Measured over com-DBLP, quantised to 16 bits per axis — a quaternary tree of depth 16 over square
cells, which is the grid the Morton order already imposes. Read *level k* as cells of side `2^k`, so
level `k` has at most `4^(16−k)` of them.

Below the scale where cells start to touch, merging four buys almost nothing — the occupied count
falls by **1.0× to 1.8×** per level, because the cells were nearly all singletons already. **From
level 9 it is exactly 4.00×.** The turn is at level 8, and it is sharp: 61,210 occupied cells out of
65,536, an occupancy of **93.4%**. That is saturation, and everything above it behaves like an image.

The cost follows immediately. The whole pyramid, every level from 0 to 16, is **1,375,552 cells —
4.34× V**, which is not a summary of anything. Taken from the saturation scale instead it is
61,210 × 4/3 = **81,613**, the `1/4 + 1/16 + …` series that `crates/fossil-sinks/src/manifest.rs, STRIDE_BITS`
already costs the tile pyramid at a third of the type. The 4/3 rule is exactly right — **once you
start at the right level**, and nothing in the data says which that is.

So the base is a knob, and here is the whole of what it trades:

| vertices per base cell | base cells | cost of the tree | marks at the finest rung |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | 61,210 | 25.7% of V | 61,210 |
| 21 | 15,303 | 6.4% of V | 15,303 |
| 83 | 3,826 | 1.6% of V | 3,826 |
| 331 | 957 | 0.4% of V | 957 |

**And the choice is not an aesthetic one — the screen makes it.** A megapixel canvas draws about
fifteen thousand marks comfortably, so a base of roughly twenty vertices per cell is the one that
fills it: the display resolution picks the row, and the corpus pays 6.4% of V for it.

**Which is why the base has to be declared in the manifest.** It is data-dependent by construction —
saturation is where *this* point set's cells start to touch, and no reader can recover it from
`vertex_count` — so a writer that does not record it has published a pyramid whose bottom step is
unknown.

Once it is declared, the rest is arithmetic: **rung `k` has `ceil(base_count / 4^k)` holons.** That
is not a projection of the measurement, it *is* the measurement — 61,210 → 15,303 → 3,826 → 957 →
240 → 60 is `ceil(n/4)` at every step, and every one of those numbers is in the table two sections
down. So `holon_count` per rung is derivable from one declared number, exactly as
`crates/fossil-sinks/src/manifest.rs, rows_at` derives a level's rows from `vertex_count` and
`crates/fossil-sinks/src/manifest.rs, planned` plans the whole pyramid before a byte is written.

**This dissolved the obstacle this page used to name, and the tree is written.** The block was
declarable and unfillable — *nothing writes one yet* — because a rung's count was something only a
completed pass could report. It is not:
`crates/fossil-sinks/src/manifest.rs, holons_at` is `ceil(vertex_count / 4^k)`, so the entire tree
is arithmetic from one declared number.

What writes it is `crates/fossil-layout/src/layout/cells.rs, Pyramid`, and the base is declared **per
vertex type** — `crates/fossil-sinks/src/manifest.rs, DEFAULT_VERTICES_PER_CELL` is sixteen, the
power of four nearest the twenty a megapixel of marks asks for, and the whole pyramid then costs 8.3%
of the type. Per type rather than globally because that is the shape the block has; the question
below is still open and what would settle it is unchanged.

<Callout title="One field of it is a measurement, and that decided the phase order">
A rung's cell count can be stated in front of the bytes. **Its quotient edge count cannot** — how
many edges survive as a quotient at a rung is measured, not planned — and a document written first
either omits it or guesses.

So the manifest is emitted **last**, over a payload that exists: `fossil_df::materialise` returns a
value and writes nothing, the layout pass writes the corpus, and
`crates/fossil-df/src/lib.rs, declare_pyramids` carries what the pass measured into the document.
That retires *the manifest is the plan and the pass is what fills it* for this block only, and the
distinction it rested on survives: a count that is arithmetic is **described** whenever it is
written, and only a measured one has an order.
</Callout>

## Three properties, in this order

### 1 · Deterministic — and this one is measured

Same corpus, same holons, bit for bit. A holon whose referent moves between writes is not
navigable: a reader who descends, pans and ascends must arrive back where they were, and a bookmark
must still resolve tomorrow.

It is the standard the tree already holds itself to elsewhere —
`crates/fossil-layout/tests/levels.rs, a_level_file_holds_exactly_what_the_level_predicate_selects`
compares written artefacts against a predicate rather than trusting the writer — and the code
already claimed it in comments: `local_moving` walks `0..n` and breaks ties strictly so the lower
community id survives, and `Weighted::contract` carries a section arguing its counting sort makes
the output bit-identical.

Claimed in comments, never run. Measured 2026-09-11, two processes per row:

| what | over | result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `crates/fossil-layout/src/layout/community.rs, community_hierarchy` | com-DBLP, 5 levels | every level's membership **and** quotient byte-identical |
| `crates/fossil-layout/src/layout/pass.rs, enrich_layout`, the whole pass | 200,000 vertices, 999,079 edges, via `crates/fossil-layout/examples/enrich_memory.rs, planted` | the entire written tree byte-identical — Morton-permuted payload, both adjacency orientations, identity index, tiles and level directories |

**The property holds.** Had it come out red, the two properties below would not be worth stating.
A spatial partition inherits it for free and for a duller reason — a cell id is a shift of a number
the payload already fixed — which is one more thing the texel reading costs nothing.

A measurement is not a guard, though, and for a while nothing kept the property holding.
`crates/fossil-layout/tests/holons.rs, the_same_graph_gives_the_same_holon_rows_twice` keeps the
**holon rows** reproducible, which is a narrower claim one artefact along: the rows come out of
`Dendrogram` in memory, and the measurement above was of `enrich_layout` writing Parquet across two
processes. `crates/fossil-layout/tests/cells.rs` narrowed the gap without closing it — it evaluates
every obligation against the written rungs, so a *wrong* summary is caught, and the same corpus
summarising *differently* twice was not.

`crates/fossil-layout/tests/cells.rs, the_same_graph_writes_byte_identical_rungs_in_a_second_process_worth_of_work` is the guard.
It spawns its own binary twice — `current_exe`, filtered to the one `#[ignore]`d half that writes where
the parent tells it to — and compares every file of the two corpora as **bytes**, rungs and
quotients alike.

Bytes because the five obligations beside it are all `count(*)` over a join, and a rung holding
exactly the right rows in a different *order* satisfies every one of them while being a different
file: the tiling is the addressing, so rows that moved between tiles are a bookmark that resolves to
somebody else's cells. Two processes because two calls inside one share every value drawn once per
process and reused — the pid, a seed behind a `OnceLock`, an address the allocator settled on — and
those are the inputs a summariser reaches for by accident.

It was run red twice before it was believed, and the second time is what says what it is for: a
per-process order over the quotient's runs, every run and every weight intact, leaves all five
obligations green and is caught here alone, at `payload/holon/r1/quotient/tiles.parquet` byte 18.

What the guard does not carry is the row above's **scale** — 4,000 vertices against 200,000 — so
what runs on every PR is the property, and the corpus it was first measured on stays a measurement.

### 2 · The branching factor is declared, not inherited

Every published step contracts by at least four — the same octave the tile pyramid already uses.
`VertexLevels` fixes that exponent in one place and says why a second spelling of it is the bug the
constant exists to prevent; a holon tree on the same octave inherits that arithmetic instead of
inventing one.

Over cells the rule is met **with equality**, which is what a mipmap is: 61,210 → 15,303 → 3,826 is
4.00× at every step. What the same rule does below saturation is the more useful half — the 1.0× to
1.8× steps fail it, so **the floor is what chooses the base**, and a writer that starts too fine
publishes a tree that does not deserialise as one rather than a tree of useless rungs.

<Callout type="warn">
**Over a dendrogram the declared factor is a floor and not a target, and that was measured rather
than intended.** This paragraph said the branching factor would be *about four everywhere*. It is
not, and it cannot be: a cut chooses out of the partitions the dendrogram already holds, and
Louvain's own steps on com-DBLP are 5.7×, 6.0× and 5.5×. There is no level near 79,270 or 13,928 to
take, so the rule that survives is **every published step contracts by at least four** — which is
what removes the 2.5× and 1.2× rungs, and is the whole of what was wrong with the emitted levels.

Getting nearer to four would need levels *between* Louvain's — undoing part of a pass's merges —
and that needs a merge ordering `community_hierarchy` does not emit. It is not a tuning knob. Over
cells the question does not arise, which is the cleanest single difference between the two
partitions after the zoom one.

`log₄(V/chunk)` is likewise an **upper bound** and not the level count: with steps of at least four
you get at most that many rungs.
</Callout>

Measured on com-DBLP at a chunk of 4,096, `crates/fossil-layout/src/layout/community.rs, Cut` keeps
three of the five levels, and that is the cut that exists in code today:

| rung | groups | ceiling it had to clear | contraction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 55,712 | 79,270 | 5.69× |
| 2 | 9,248 | 13,928 | 6.02× |
| 3 | 1,696 | 2,312 | 5.45× |

Levels 4 and 5 are dropped because neither is a quarter of the one above it — which is exactly the
1.2× step that moved modularity by 0.0001. **The tree has no root**, and the cut does not invent
one: it ends where the dendrogram stops delivering.
`crates/fossil-layout/src/layout/community.rs, flatten_to_budget` is the existing half of this: it
walks the hierarchy and picks **one** level that fits a budget; `Cut` is the same walk choosing a
**sequence**. A quaternary tree ends the other way round — at one cell, whose members are the graph.

**Which end a rung sequence has read as immaterial here, and it is the thing that decided the
question.** This paragraph ended *it is the one thing the manifest block does not need to know,
because it declares counts and not a method* — true of the manifest, and allowed to stand in for the
whole question. The manifest does not need to know; a reader descending does, because a cut that
ends at 1,696 groups is a coarse view that stops existing above the level pyramid's own floor of
1,239 marks, where a quaternary keeps quartering the mark count past it — all the way to one, and
measurably rather than on paper, which is
[six rungs further and not one](/docs/design/cells#the-holarchy-does-not-reach-further-and-the-margin-is-six-rungs-nothing-asks-for).
That the six are below any budget anything spends is the same section's second half.
[One multiscale structure](/docs/design/cells#the-corpus-carries-one-multiscale-structure-and-it-is-the-cell-pyramid)
is where that is weighed.

**And the floor is checkable from the document alone.** Because a rung declares a count rather than
a scale, `crates/fossil-sinks/src/manifest.rs, contracts_by_at_least` evaluates the whole rule over
the manifest — every rung against the one below it, the finest against the type's own
`vertex_count` — without opening a file. The factor it compares with is `VertexLevels::stride` at
one and never a literal four, so the octave keeps the single spelling that constant exists to
enforce. A reader weighing a descent finds out it is not worth taking before it pays for the rung.

### 3 · A holon is defined against the level below, so it is checkable

This is the half the change of mind does not touch. The bar is the one `levels.rs` already clears —
evaluate the definition against the data and diff, rather than trusting the writer. For a holon
there are three obligations and all three are falsifiable by symmetric difference:

<div className="fd-steps">

**Membership.** The members of a holon are *exactly* the children that name it as parent. Not a
superset, not what the writer recorded separately.

**Count.** Its member count is the sum of its children's counts — and this is an obligation on the
**row** rather than on the data. It only has content because the count is recorded beside the
members: derive it from the member set and the check is a tautology no corruption can fail.

**Edges.** A child edge whose two ends have **different** parents sums into an edge between those
parents. A child edge whose two ends have the **same** parent is not an edge of that parent at all
— it is absorbed into the parent's internal weight.

</div>

**None of the three mentions how the partition was chosen**, which is why
`crates/fossil-layout/tests/holons.rs, the_three_obligations_hold_up_a_cut_of_a_partition_nobody_designed`
already ran them over a partition with no design behind it and they held. A grid is such a partition.
*The partition is arbitrary, the aggregation is exact* is the sentence those tests were written
under before the page had it.

<Callout type="warn">
**That third bullet said "its edges are the aggregation of its children's edges", and that is
false as a union.** It reads as *relabel the endpoints and keep the set*, which emits self-loops
where a quotient has none — and on a graph with any community structure it is not an edge case but
the overwhelming majority. Measured on the planted fixture
`crates/fossil-layout/tests/holons.rs` uses — 256 vertices, 1,095 edges — **1,088 of the 1,095 are
absorbed** and the aggregate quotient has **seven**.

The check that catches both mistakes is **mass conservation per rung**: cross weight plus internal
weight equals the edge count of the level below, at every rung. A writer that drops sibling edges
and one that keeps them as self-loops both fail it, and no count of holons notices either.
</Callout>

**The base case has to be stated or the chain does not reach the graph.** *Defined against the
level below* bottoms out at the finest rung, whose children are the vertices themselves and whose
quotient is the raw edge list. Without that, the obligations relate each published level to another
published level and nothing anchors the sequence to the corpus.

**And the quotient is an artefact beside the rows, not a column on one.** A holon row is one holon;
a quotient edge is a pair of them, which is a different arity — the same reason a corpus writes its
vertices and its relations as separate artefacts rather than as one wide table.

In the manifest it is `crates/fossil-sinks/src/manifest.rs, HolonQuotient`, under the rung's own
path, declaring its edge count for the reason a relation declares one — a tile with no rows is not
written, so a 404 reads as *no edges here* and as *never uploaded* alike. It is **optional per
rung**, and the next section is what a writer answers that with. What is deliberately *not* in the
manifest is a summed weight: mass conservation is a property of the bytes, and a total recorded here
would be a third place to disagree rather than a fourth thing to check.

## No coarse quotient is drawable, and the partition is not the reason

The page used to end on *whether the aggregated edge set is worth writing at every level*, with a
hairball at level 5 as the worry. It is not a worry and it is not about Louvain. Measured, spatial
partition over the same com-DBLP:

| level | cells | edges absorbed | quotient edges | mean degree | density |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6 | 70,462 | 50.2% | 246,412 | 7.0 | 0.010% |
| 8 | 61,210 | 59.6% | 213,128 | 7.0 | 0.011% |
| 9 | 15,303 | 64.4% | 153,880 | 20.1 | 0.13% |
| 10 | 3,826 | 70.0% | 110,621 | 57.8 | 1.51% |
| 11 | 957 | 74.5% | 70,416 | 147.2 | 15.4% |
| 12 | 240 | 78.1% | 22,899 | 190.8 | 79.8% |
| 13 | 60 | 81.5% | 1,770 | 59.0 | 100% |

At level 12 the quotient is **79.8% of a complete graph**, and at 13 it is complete. Nothing drawn
from it carries information: every node is joined to every other, and the picture is the same
picture for any graph of sixty nodes.

**And it is worse than the community quotient at every comparable size**, which is the measurement
that rules out blaming the partition. Against the community rows in the Louvain table — 9,248 groups
at mean degree 11.6, 1,696 at 23.5, 595 at 23.2 — the spatial cells are **2.5× to 6× denser** at
comparable counts: 3,826 cells at 57.8 against 1,696 communities at 23.5, and 957 cells at 147.2
against 595 communities at 23.2. The reason is mechanical. A community hierarchy **nests**, so
merging two groups absorbs the edges between them — that is what the modularity was maximising.
Merging four neighbouring cells absorbs only what happens to fall inside, which is why the
absorbed column climbs so much more slowly than the node count falls.

<Callout type="warn">
**That sentence said *four **arbitrary** neighbouring cells*, and the word is retracted.** It is the
same false framing the decision above retracts: a cell is not arbitrary with respect to the graph,
because the id axis it is cut on is monotone in group order and a group order is a depth-first walk
of the dendrogram. Nothing in the density measurement depended on the word — what it shows is
narrower and survives whole: **a merge that maximises modularity absorbs edges on purpose, and a
fixed-count cut absorbs whatever its boundaries happen to enclose.** Nesting is the mechanism, not
blindness, and the numbers in the table are unchanged.
</Callout>

So the conclusion is not about this corpus and not about this partition: **no coarse quotient of a
real graph is drawable, however it is partitioned.** An image has no edges. A mipmap summarises a
*field*, and a field is exactly the thing that averages; a relation is not, and a coarse level of a
relation is a complete graph.

Which says where edges go. **They are drawn at the bottom**, where the quotient has mean degree 7.0
and density 0.010% — which is where a three-pixel floor is already drawing them, because at that
scale the marks are far enough apart to have something between them. And the quotient at a coarse
rung is kept **to be measured against**: it is what makes mass conservation checkable per rung. It
stops being an input to a renderer, which is a demotion and not a deletion — `HolonQuotient` is
still optional per rung, and a writer that publishes one is publishing evidence rather than a
picture.

### The quotient saturates down the pyramid, and the marks do not

The table above is over the 16-bit quaternary of positions, which is the tree the Morton order
imposes rather than the one the writer emits. Over the **written** rungs the same saturation is
there, and it arrives sooner. Measured on com-DBLP by
`crates/fossil-layout/tests/level_vs_rung.rs, what_a_level_costs_and_what_a_rung_costs_on_com_dblp`:

| rung | cells | quotient density | bytes per mark, at the whole extent | fidelity: TV against a null of the same mark count, each on a grid fitted to that count |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 19,818 | 0.14% | **69.0** | 0.2624 against 0.3580 — **+0.0956** |
| 2 | 4,955 | 1.34% | 115.3 | 0.2513 against 0.3681 — **+0.1168** |
| 3 | 1,239 | **12.24%** | 213.9 | 0.2339 against 0.3657 — **+0.1318** |
| 4 | 310 | **69.92%** | **285.5** | 0.2230 against 0.3700 — **+0.1470** |
| 5 | 78 | 98.83% | 144.5 | 0.1754 against 0.3567 — **+0.1814** |
| 6 | 20 | 100% | 133.9 | 0.1798 against 0.4341 — **+0.2543** |
| 7 | 5 | 100% | 110.2 | 0.1651 against 0.4992 — **+0.3341** |
| 8 | 2 | 100% | 183.5 | 0.5742 against 0.4992 — **−0.0751**, the one row above one bin per mark |
| 9 | 1 | none left | 222.0 | 0.7475 against 0.7475 — a tie on a four-bin grid, which is no reading at all |

**The quotient saturates.** By rung 4 it is seven-tenths of a complete graph and by rung 5 it is the
complete graph, which is the same thing the table above says at its levels 12 and 13 — a relation
whose coarse level joins everything to everything. That column is a property of the *edges* and it is
unaffected by anything below.

**And the bytes go the wrong way while it happens.** Bytes per mark should fall as an artefact
coarsens — a coarser rung is fewer rows for the same picture. Through rung 4 it climbs instead, 69.0
→ 115.3 → 213.9 → 285.5, because the quotient is filling in faster than the cell count is falling.
It only falls again at rung 5, once there is almost nothing left to write.

**What does not go with either of them is the marks**, and the last column is a retraction.

<Callout type="warn">
**This section used to read «and the deep rungs fail on their own terms, which the quotient already
predicted», and report rungs 4 through 9 scoring worse than an unstructured subsample of their own
size. That was a measurement of the ruler.** Total variation was taken on the fixed 48×48 grid
`/docs/design/camera` states — 2,304 bins, which at rung 4's 310 marks is **7.43 bins per mark**.
Neither the rung nor its null can reach two thirds of the cells, both distances pin against 1
(0.8431 and 0.8415), and the −0.0016 between them is binning noise. The signature was already
visible in the published table: the gap collapsed from ~0.1 to ~0.002 and then held at ~0.002 for six
rungs running, which is what a saturated statistic looks like and not what losing information looks
like. [A fidelity measure has a resolution](/docs/design/camera#a-fidelity-measure-has-a-resolution-and-below-one-bin-per-mark-it-is-measuring-itself)
is the general rule, now stated beside the measure it qualifies.

Re-measured on a grid fitted to each arm, `crates/fossil-layout/tests/level_vs_rung.rs, fidelity`
gets the column above: **every rung beats its null, and the margin grows down the pyramid** rather
than decaying — +0.0956 at rung 1 to +0.3341 at rung 7. That is what count-weighted centroids should
do against an unstructured subsample. A rung carries the exact mass of its members and has no
sampling noise at all; its null has nothing else, and the null's noise grows as the sample shrinks.
`crates/fossil-layout/tests/level_vs_rung.rs, resolution_sweep` is the evidence and it is kept
rather than replaced by its conclusion: across both corpora at five resolutions, **no arm measured
at one bin per mark or finer fails, and every failure sits at two bins per mark or coarser** — 130
rows, 72 of them in range, zero counterexamples either way.

**Rung 8 is the one honest failure, and it is the ruler again.** Two marks fit `sqrt(2)`, which
floors to a 2×2 grid, so rung 8 is measured at exactly **2.00 bins per mark** — the only fitted row
still out of range. Rung 9 is one mark on four bins and its tie is arithmetic rather than evidence.

**And the two corpora stop disagreeing.** On the old ruler the planted fixture passed rungs 7 and 8 —
by +0.0019 and +0.0010, which is the noise floor — and failed 4, 5, 6 and 9, where com-DBLP failed
all six below rung 3; nothing explained the difference and nothing could, because both tables were
readings of the same saturation. On the fitted grid both corpora pass rungs 1 through 7, both fail
only rung 8, and both pass rung 9 on a grid too coarse to mean anything. The disagreement was the
grid.
</Callout>

One claim that stood on the old ruler does **not** survive the fitted one, and it is the reverse of a
retraction — it was too generous to the rung. The page said that at every mark count where both
answer, the rung is closer to the payload than the level drawing the same marks. **That holds at two
of the three counts and reverses at the finest.** On the fitted grid, at 19,818 marks level 2 scores
0.2460 against rung 1's 0.2624; at 4,955 the rung wins, 0.2513 against 0.2696; at 1,239 the rung wins
by more, 0.2339 against 0.2804. The planted fixture reproduces the same crossing. The mechanism is
visible once the resolution is: **a centroid concentrates its cell's whole mass at one point**, which
is right at a scale coarser than the cell and wrong at a scale finer than it, while a sample spreads
the same mass out. So *a summary beats a sample* is a claim about the scale it is judged at — the
rung's advantage is a coarse-scale advantage, and it widens exactly as the picture coarsens.

What this does **not** settle is whether the writer should stop emitting the deep rungs — not because
they carry nothing, which is now measured false, but because nothing ever asks for them.
[What this does not settle](/docs/design/cells#what-this-does-not-settle) carries that, with what it
would cost.

## A categorical channel gets a mode and a purity

A texel averages colour. A category does not average: the mean of two `cluster_id`s is not a cluster,
and `crates/fossil-sinks/src/generated.rs, PAYLOAD_CATEGORICAL` is the column that says which of the
payload's columns this is true of.

**That constant is a fact about fossil's writer, not about the corpus in front of you**, and until
the channel was declared it was the closest thing to an answer the document had. A cell row's `mode`
is a bare `uint32`. `crates/fossil-sinks/src/manifest.rs, HolonRung` said *the mode and purity of the
payload's categorical* in prose, `corpus.bnf`'s `mode` role said *the majority value of a categorical
over the rows summarised*, and neither named **which** categorical — because until
[`channels:`](/docs/design/position) there was nothing to name. So a reader colouring a rung by
`mode` had to guess which palette the values belonged to, and could not know the domain at all. That
is the same defect the `domain` field closed one level down, and it survived at this one.

`crates/fossil-sinks/src/manifest.rs, HolonTree` names it now — `mode_channel:`, a scalar sitting
between `coordinates:` and `rungs:` — and the shape comes from the field this tree already carries
for the same reason. A holon's position is declared rather than derived in silence, and the section
below notes that it *does a second job while it is there: it names which columns hold the position*.
The mode had no such field. It has one, and what it holds is the **name** of a channel, resolved
against the vertex type's own `channels:` list.

**A reference and not a copy**, which is the whole of why it is a name. Repeating the channel's
`domain` on the tree would put a measured number in the document twice, and a number written twice is
the thing this block was added to stop — the second copy is the one that goes stale when the
partition moves. A reader follows one hop and gets the column and the domain from the entry that
measured them.

**Two states, and that is a departure worth stating rather than noticing.** `coordinates:` and
`channels:` are lists with three states, because *this type carries none* is an answer a list can
give. A reference cannot give it: a rung's `mode` column always holds the mode of something, so
there is no «carries none» to express. And the two are not a choice a writer makes — every tree
`fossil-layout` writes names its channel, so the absent state is reachable only by a tree written
before the field or by a writer that is not this one. Absent means *nobody said*, and it never means
*the mode is of nothing*, because no such rung exists.

**The reference is checked in two places, and neither could make the other's claim.** A name that
resolves to nothing is the failure a reference introduces, so `apps/corpus/guards` carries
`mode-names-a-channel` over the document — the name is one of the type's own channels, and the entry
it lands on is `categorical`, because a measure has no majority to take. That guard cannot reach
bytes. `crates/fossil-layout/tests/cells.rs` can and does: the pyramid's fifth obligation no longer
spells `cluster_id`, it *resolves* the reference and recounts the mode and the purity over whatever
column it lands on, against the written Parquet. So a tree that named the wrong channel fails a test
rather than drawing a picture, and a tree that named a channel nobody declared fails a guard.

The answer is the one indexed textures already use — **the majority value, and what fraction of the
cell holds it**. Both, because the mode without the purity is a lie at the coarse end. Measured,
purity of a cell with respect to `cluster_id`:

| level | purity of the mode |
| --- | --- |
| ≤ 8 | 100% |
| 9 | 98.6% |
| 10 | 94.0% |
| 11 | 83.4% |
| 12 | 68.1% |
| 13 | 48.7% |
| 16 | 7.7% |

At and below the saturation scale a cell is **pure by construction** — it holds one cluster, and the
mode is not a summary but the value. It decays smoothly from there, and the decay is the useful
signal: a cell whose mode holds 94% of it draws solid, one at 27% draws desaturated, and the picture
then shows **where the structure is sharp and where the reader is looking at a blur**. Which is
strictly more than a colour would have carried, and it costs one extra column on a holon row.

## And the one thing that is not an exact aggregate

Counts sum. Edges sum. **Where a holon goes on the plane is a choice** — and a spatial partition
narrows that choice without closing it. A cell's extent is known from its id alone, so the two
candidates are the cell's own centre and the members' centroid; they are different pictures, the
first regular and the second following the data, and no amount of checking makes either correct.

So it is declared rather than derived in silence, exactly as a vertex's position is:
[where x and y come from](/docs/design/position) is the field, and a holon's position carries a
provenance in it like any other. Everything else here is verified; this one is declared.

It is the same type and not a second one — `crates/fossil-sinks/src/manifest.rs, CoordinateSystem`
on the tree, with `crates/fossil-sinks/src/manifest.rs, Provenance`'s three answers available — and
it does a second job while it is there: it names *which columns* hold the position. A holon's is
`derived` by construction, which makes the deriver the part that earns its keep, because re-deriving
is how a reader checks that a holon's referent has not moved and nothing can re-run what nothing
names. A tree whose writer recorded none reads back as **not declared**, which is weaker than
`derived` rather than equivalent to it — the rule a corpus written before the block existed is
already held under, and the reason the whole block is optional rather than defaulted.

## The name promises a nesting this does not have

**«Holon» is Koestler's**, and the word means a thing that is simultaneously a whole and a part —
semantic nesting, a level of organisation that is *about* something. That is exactly what the
sentence at the top of this page gives up. If the partition carries no meaning, a name that promises
meaning works against every reader who looks it up.

The candidate is `holon` → **`cell`**, which says what it is: a region of the plane, summarised.
`pyramid:` is then free as a block name if the rungs are ever retired into the level machinery.
Against it: a cost, which stood here as an estimate — *a manifest block, a crate's public types, a
test file and this page* — and is counted below instead. **The estimate was wrong in both
directions**, and the decision is still not taken, because what is left is not arithmetic about the
word.

**The condition this paragraph named has been met, and the paragraph is rewritten rather than
annotated.** It said the rename waited on *a writer that partitions spatially, so that nothing in
the tree is a community any more*, and until one existed the old name was still describing what
`crates/fossil-layout/src/layout/community.rs, Cut` emits. That writer exists — the pass writes
`crates/fossil-layout/src/layout/cells.rs, Pyramid`, and `Cut` is reached only from tests and from
`crates/fossil-layout/examples/hierarchy_stats.rs, main` — and the decision above closes the
question the writer was waiting on. So nothing in a written tree is a community, and the name
describes a thing that no longer exists in the artefact.

What is left holding the rename is its cost, and the three sections below are that cost measured.
**It is not a scheduling question.** Half of it is already paid; the half that is left is a manifest
key whose rename fails *silently* rather than loudly; and the exit this page had assumed was
disqualifying turns out not to be.

### Half of it is already paid, and the half that is paid is the half a reader sees

`corpus.bnf` writes this pyramid's columns as `cell_id`, `count`, `internal`, `mode` and `purity`,
in a container it names `cell`, and the quotient's as `src_cell` and `dst_cell`. Both generated
projections of that file — `crates/fossil-sinks/src/generated.rs` and
`packages/corpus/src/vocabulary.generated.ts` — carry **zero** occurrences of `holon`. So the column
vocabulary a reader meets in a Parquet file is already `cell`, and it never said `holon`: what
`cargo xtask corpus` generates cannot be part of this rename because it was never part of the old
name.

What is left is the manifest block, the Rust types above it, the prose around it, and one directory
name.

### The rest, counted

**561 occurrences in 36 files**, case-insensitively, over everything `git ls-files` lists. Split by
what a rename would actually have to do to each:

| where the word is | occurrences | files | what a rename is there |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rust code — `HolonTree`, `HolonRung`, `HolonQuotient`, `HOLON_PREFIX`, `holon_count`, `holons_at`, `with_holons` and the test fns | 260 | 11 | mechanical, and the compiler finds every one |
| Rust doc and line comments | 162 | 19 | prose, and the compiler finds none of it |
| design pages on this site | 109 | 6 | prose |
| guard code under `apps/corpus/guards/` | 11 | 3 | **two of these read the manifest key** |
| TypeScript and JavaScript comments | 8 | 5 | prose |
| a corpus on disk — the conformance manifest and one snapshot | 7 | 2 | **the format change, and the whole of it** |
| `corpus.bnf` | 3 | 1 | prose; its columns are already `cell` |
| `apps/docs/content/docs/design/meta.json` | 1 | 1 | this page's slug |

**And one more that no count of the word reaches: this page's own route.** `/docs/design/cells` is
cited **50 times in 20 files**. Twenty-two of them, in six files, are pages of this site, where
`content.test.ts` fails the build on a `/docs/…` link that does not resolve. The other **28, in
fourteen files**, are in Rust doc comments, in `corpus.bnf` and in `packages/corpus/src/corpus.ts`,
and nothing in either half of the tree checks those. A rename that moves the page and not the route
is the exact failure this site's guards were built out of, one directory along.

### The wire is four spellings, and only two of them are a compatibility question

Four spellings reach a corpus on disk. Two are keys — `holons:` on the vertex info and `holon_count:`
on a rung — and two are values: the `holon/` prefix and the `holon` coordinate system.

**The values are not a compatibility question at all.**
`crates/fossil-sinks/src/manifest.rs, HOLON_PREFIX` is *written into* `prefix:`, and every reader
takes the declared prefix rather than the constant — which is what `Projection::path`'s own
paragraph means by declared rather than conventional. A corpus written last month keeps resolving
its tiles under `holon/` however the writer spells the next one, and a corpus is free to carry a
directory whose name matches nothing in any crate.

**The keys are, and they break in opposite directions — which is the finding.**

<Callout type="warn" title="Renaming `holons:` does not stop an old corpus loading. It stops it saying anything.">
`crates/fossil-sinks/src/manifest.rs, VertexInfo` carries the block behind
`#[serde(default, skip_serializing_if = "Option::is_none")]`, and **no struct in the manifest model
sets `deny_unknown_fields`**. So a corpus written before the rename deserialises *cleanly* under the
new name, with the pyramid **silently absent** — not an error, not a state a reader can tell apart
from a corpus that genuinely declares no tree, because
`crates/fossil-sinks/src/manifest.rs, a_vertex_written_before_the_holon_block_existed_declares_nothing`
asserts that absence is legal and means *declares nothing*. The rungs are still on disk under
`holon/` and nothing points at them.

`holon_count` is the opposite — required, no default, so a stale rung fails loudly with a missing
field. **Under a rename with no alias that failure is unreachable**, because the block above it was
dropped in silence first. The loud half is the half that never fires.

**This tree has already paid that exact failure once.** `crates/fossil-df-wasm/src/lib.rs` carries
the account: the browser's report of a run silently lost `holons:` and then `channels:` — two fields
present in the YAML the very same call emitted — and what caught it was somebody reading both, not a
test.
</Callout>

The guards inherit the silence rather than catching it. `apps/corpus/guards/inspect.mjs, holonsOf`
reads `info.holons` and returns `null` for an absent key, and the `mode-names-a-channel` guard in
`apps/corpus/guards/guards.mjs` reads that `null` as *declares no holon tree, so there is no
reference to resolve* — a **note, not a failure**. A rename with no alias turns that guard vacuous
over every corpus written before it, and vacuously passing is how a guard stops guarding.

### The two exits, and the first is cheaper than this page assumed

**`#[serde(alias = "holons")]` does not leave the document saying the old name.** That objection is
the obvious one and it is false for the mechanism actually available: `alias` is **read-only**. The
writer emits the new key from the first corpus after the change; only the reader still accepts the
old one. What keeps the old spelling is one attribute per renamed key and the corpora already
written — which are exactly the set no rename can reach. The cost is two attributes, and what it
buys is that the silent-absence failure above cannot happen.

Its real price is the one an alias always has: **two spellings of one key are legal forever**, which
is a statement about the format and not about a crate, and this site does not say anywhere whether
that is allowed. `apps/corpus/guards/inspect.mjs, holonsOf` is the one function in the tree that
reads the key, so it is the only place the duality would have to live — but a conformance corpus
then has to decide whether a document spelling the old key is conformant, and that is a question for
`/docs/format`, not for this page.

**A migration costs the alias plus a tool that does not exist.** Nothing in the tree rewrites a
written manifest: `crates/fossil-cli/src/main.rs, Commands` has four subcommands and none of them
opens a corpus to edit it. So «what does a reader do with a corpus written last month» has, today,
exactly one answer — *nothing, in silence* — and a migration is the option that has to build the
answer rather than choose one.

### What this needs in order to be taken

Not a further measurement of the word. Three things, and the first is the whole of it:

1. **A ruling on whether two spellings of a manifest key may be legal at once.** If yes, the alias is
   two attributes and the rename is otherwise mechanical. If no, the rename waits on a migration
   tool and is a format change with a version bump behind it.
2. **Whether the page route moves with the block.** 28 of its 50 citations are outside this site and
   nothing checks them, so moving the route and moving the key are separable decisions that have
   been treated as one.
3. **Somewhere for the format policy to be stated**, because 1 is not a fact about this pyramid. The
   same question is open one artefact along for
   [`version: gar/v1`](/docs/design/corpus#the-version-string-names-a-format-this-page-declares-non-conformant),
   and answering it twice differently is the thing to avoid.

## What this does not settle

**Whether the writer should stop emitting the rungs nothing asks for.**

<Callout type="warn">
**This question was posed as «whether the writer should stop emitting the rungs that fail fidelity»,
and there are none.** The six failures it named were an artefact of measuring 310 marks on a
2,304-bin grid; re-measured on a grid fitted to each arm, every rung beats an unstructured subsample
of its own size and the margin widens as the pyramid coarsens. The question survives the retraction
because a second measurement from the same instrument puts it on firmer ground than the first one
did — **not «these rungs carry nothing», which is now measured false, but «nothing ever asks for
them», which the tree states in three places.**
</Callout>

A camera spends a budget in *marks* and takes the coarsest artefact that still carries them, so the
floor on what anything is read at is the smallest budget anything spends — not the deepest zoom, since
zooming in shrinks what the rectangle holds and answers level 0 off the payload.
`crates/fossil-layout/tests/level_vs_rung.rs, asked_for` collects the three budgets that exist in
this tree: **15,000** marks (`SCREEN_MARKS`, the number the pyramid's own base is picked with),
**20,000** (`BOUNDED_DEFAULTS.limit`, what `apps/playground/src/tiles.ts` actually spends), and
**160,000** (a 1,200 px canvas at three pixels a mark, if the door ever took the canvas). The
smallest decides, and on com-DBLP **only rung 1, at 19,818 cells, is above it**. Eight of the nine
rungs are below anything's budget, and they are **958 kB of the cell pyramid's 2,322** — 41% of it,
against the 119 kB the fidelity reading would have saved.

**And the obvious conclusion is still not taken here, because the property it would break is
load-bearing.** `crates/fossil-sinks/src/manifest.rs, planned` emits a complete list — rung 1 up to
the rung holding a single cell — and that completeness is what lets everything downstream stay
arithmetic. A reader computes `ceil(base_count / 4^k)` and knows the rung is there; nothing needs a
*this rung was not written* path, and a corpus fossil wrote always has the artefact its own
arithmetic names. A writer that stopped at a measured floor would make the tree's tail
data-dependent — exactly the property
[the decision above](/docs/design/cells#the-corpus-carries-one-multiscale-structure-and-it-is-the-cell-pyramid)
refused to pay for in a holarchy — and a budget is a worse floor to cut at than a fidelity score
would have been, because it is a property of a *reader* and not of the corpus: the writer would be
choosing what to emit out of a constant living on the other side of the wasm boundary. The tension is
real in both directions and nothing in the tree resolves it. What would settle it is a reader that
descends past rung 1 and says which it would rather have: a rung nothing on its side will ever ask
for, or an absence it has to handle.

**Whether the base is declared per corpus or once globally.** The measurements say it is
data-dependent and must be written down; they do not say where. Per vertex type is the shape the
block already has, and a global default would be a number in a spec that is wrong for most corpora —
but a corpus of many small types would then carry the same measurement many times. What would
settle it is a second corpus of a different shape, measured the same way.

**The name.** Above, counted, with the ruling it waits on.

**Which level a reader opens on.** The tree makes every level addressable and says nothing about
where a reader starts. The screen picks the base; nothing yet picks the entry point.

**How a holon tree is written incrementally.** Everything above assumes one pass over a finished
corpus, and a spatial partition makes this harder rather than easier in one specific way: a new
vertex outside the current bounding box requantises every position and renumbers every `dense_id`.

**Which relation a tree is over when a type has several.** The block names them and the writer
chooses them; nothing here says what a reader should do with two trees over the same type, and
`relations` is a list because refusing to answer that is cheaper than answering it wrongly.

**What a guard would have to grow to read a rung.** `apps/corpus/guards/manifest.mjs` is a line
scanner that reads a nested mapping one level deep and skips what is deeper, so it sees the tree's
`prefix` and `chunk_size` and not its rungs — the same wall `index:` hit, and the same fix.
Nothing reads a rung today, which is why it is a design question and not a gap.
